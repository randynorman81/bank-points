Cell Division and Cell Transport

The 5 big ideas

  1. DNA is the cell's instruction book. Before a cell divides, it copies that book.
  2. Mitosis makes two identical body cells. Meiosis makes sex cells (gametes) with half the chromosomes.
  3. The cell membrane is a gatekeeper. It lets some things in and keeps others out.
  4. Passive transport is free: stuff moves from crowded to less crowded.
  5. Active transport costs energy (ATP): stuff is pushed from less crowded to more crowded.
Study plan: do one part a night, then do the practice. When you can finish the quiz without peeking, you are ready.

Part 1: DNA, chromatin, and chromosomes

Chromatin is loose, uncoiled DNA found in the nucleus during interphase (like a bowl of thin noodles). Chromosomes are DNA packed tightly into the X shape, seen only during cell division. Same DNA, different shape, like a rope that is stretched out when you use it and wound up when you carry it.

A duplicated chromosome has copied itself. It is two identical sister chromatids joined at the centromere.

Homologous chromosomes are matching pairs, one from each parent, with the same genes. Autosomes are the 22 pairs that are not sex chromosomes. The 23rd pair is X and Y.

Cytokinesis splits the cytoplasm into two separate cells. Genetic continuity: S phase copies the DNA, mitosis splits it evenly, cytokinesis splits the cell, so both new cells have the same complete DNA. This is how you grow and heal a cut.

Sperm (23) + egg (23) = zygote (46). Development goes: Zygote, Blastocyst, Stem cell, Cell differentiation. Every cell has the same DNA. Cells look different because they switch on different genes.

Part 4: The cell membrane

The membrane is the security guard at the door. Its job is homeostasis: keeping the inside steady. It is selectively permeable (semipermeable): it lets some things pass and blocks others. It is a double layer of phospholipids with proteins embedded in it.

Part 5: Passive transport (no energy)

A concentration gradient is a difference in how much of something there is. Passive transport goes downhill, from high to low, and costs no ATP.

Part 6: Active and bulk transport (energy needed)

Active transport goes uphill, from low to high, using ATP and pump proteins (the Na+/K+ pump). Big things move in vesicles: endocytosis (Endo = in, the membrane wraps around material) and exocytosis (Exo = exit, a vesicle fuses with the membrane and releases contents, like insulin).

Key vocabulary (38 terms)

TermWhat it means
MitosisThe process where the nucleus of a body cell divides into two identical nuclei. It has four stages: prophase, metaphase, anaphase, telophase.
BlastocystA hollow ball of cells made when the zygote divides again and again by mitosis in early development.
Cell differentiationWhen cells become specialized for a job (like nerve, muscle, or skin) by using different parts of the same DNA.
Stem cellAn unspecialized cell that can still become many different types of cells.
Cell cycleThe repeating life cycle of a cell: interphase (G1, S, G2), then mitosis, then cytokinesis.
InterphaseThe longest part of the cell cycle, when the cell grows, copies its DNA, and gets ready to divide (G1, S, G2).
CytokinesisThe division of the cytoplasm that pinches one cell into two separate cells.
ChromatinDNA that is loose and uncoiled, found in the nucleus during interphase.
ChromosomeDNA that is tightly packed and coiled into a visible shape, seen during cell division.
Duplicated chromosomeA chromosome that has copied itself: two identical sister chromatids joined at the centromere (an X shape).
Sister chromatidsThe two identical copies of a chromosome that make up a duplicated chromosome.
CentromereThe spot in the middle that holds the two sister chromatids together.
CentrosomeA cell structure that moves to opposite poles during prophase and anchors the spindle fibers.
Spindle fibersThin protein threads that attach to the chromosomes and pull the sister chromatids apart.
ProphaseFirst stage of mitosis: chromatin condenses into chromosomes, the nuclear envelope breaks down, centrosomes move to the poles, and spindle fibers form.
MetaphaseSecond stage: spindle fibers line the chromosomes up in a single row along the middle (equator) of the cell.
AnaphaseThird stage: the sister chromatids are pulled apart at the centromere toward opposite poles.
TelophaseFourth stage: nuclear membranes re-form around the two sets of chromosomes, the chromosomes uncoil, and the spindle fibers dissolve.
Genetic continuity (mitosis)Each new cell gets the same complete set of DNA as the parent cell, so the genetic information stays unchanged.
GameteA sex cell (sperm or egg). Gametes are haploid.
Somatic cellAny body cell that is not a gamete, like skin, liver, or muscle. Somatic cells are diploid.
AutosomesThe 22 pairs of chromosomes that are not the sex chromosomes.
Homologous chromosomesA matching pair of chromosomes, one from the mother and one from the father, carrying the same genes.
Diploid cell (2n)A cell with two full sets of chromosomes. In humans, 46.
Haploid cell (n)A cell with one full set of chromosomes. In humans, 23.
ZygoteThe first cell of a new organism, made when a sperm and an egg join. It is diploid.
Cell membraneThe thin outer boundary of the cell that controls what enters and leaves. It is made of a double layer of phospholipids with proteins embedded in it.
HomeostasisKeeping the conditions inside the cell (or body) steady and balanced.
SemipermeableLets some substances pass through while blocking others.
Selectively permeableDescribes the membrane choosing which substances pass. In class, it means the same idea as semipermeable.
Passive transportMovement across the membrane that needs no energy (no ATP). Things move from high to low concentration.
ConcentrationHow much of a substance is in a given amount of space or solution.
Concentration gradientThe difference in concentration between two areas.
DiffusionThe movement of a substance from an area of high concentration to an area of low concentration until it is spread out evenly.
Simple diffusionSmall, uncharged molecules pass directly through the membrane, with no protein help.
Facilitated diffusionLarge or charged molecules cross the membrane down their concentration gradient through channel or carrier proteins. No ATP needed.
Channel proteinA protein that forms a tunnel through the membrane so certain molecules or ions can pass through.
OsmosisThe diffusion of water across a selectively permeable membrane, toward the side with more dissolved solute.

Memory tricks

  • Interphase: G1, S, G2 = Grow, Synthesize (copy DNA), Get ready.
  • Mitosis: PMAT. Metaphase = Middle. Anaphase = Apart.
  • Chromatin = loose. Chromosomes = tight X.
  • Hyper = more solute outside, cell shrinks. Hypo = less solute outside, cell swells.
  • Passive = free, downhill. Active = ATP, uphill.
  • Endo = in. Exo = exit.
  • Mitosis = copies. Meiosis = half-sets.
