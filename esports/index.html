Same idea as any other GHSA-sanctioned sport — just played on a screen instead of a field.

GHSA Esports

The Georgia High School Association (GHSA) — the same organization that runs football, basketball, and every other sanctioned sport in the state — officially sponsors esports. Schools field teams, students earn a spot on a roster, and there's a real state championship at the end of each season.

Matches are played after school, usually once a week per game, from a school's computer lab or esports room (or sometimes from home, depending on the school).

PlayVS

PlayVS is the company GHSA partners with to actually run the league: scheduling matches, tracking standings, verifying rosters, and streaming games. Your coach sets your team up on PlayVS, and matches happen through the platform.

PlayVS runs scholastic esports leagues in most U.S. states, so the same platform is used whether you're playing a Georgia opponent or (in some formats) a team from another state.

Games you can play

GHSA offers 12 titles split across a Fall championship season, a Spring championship season, and additional titles available year-round.

League of Legends

Fall

5v5 team battle arena. Roster needs at least 4 players to compete; teams typically carry 5 starters plus 1–2 subs.

Match day: Tuesdays

Fall

Individual 1v1 matches (each roster player fights their own opponent); a doubles 2v2 format also exists. Divisions are split by school classification (A–2A, 3A–4A, 5A–6A).

Fall

Fall 2026 season

GHSA runs two seasons per school year — Fall (League of Legends, Smash, Mario Kart) and Spring (Rocket League, Splatoon, Marvel Rivals). Here's what's confirmed for Fall 2026.

How to get on a team

You don't need to be a "pro" — most teams are built from scratch every season.

1

Find your school's coach

Esports is usually run by a teacher/sponsor (often in the CS, media, or tech department). Ask front office or your CS teacher who runs it at your school.

2

Check eligibility

Same GHSA eligibility rules as any other sport: enrolled full-time, meeting academic/attendance requirements, and any school-specific forms (physical/insurance requirements are typically waived for esports, but confirm with your coach).

3

Pick your game

Try out for whichever title your school offers and you're strongest in — you can only be on the roster for games your school has set up on PlayVS.

4

Get added to PlayVS

Your coach adds you to the team roster in PlayVS with your school email. That's how you'll see your schedule and join matches.

Frequently asked questions

Does esports count as a real GHSA sport?

Yes. GHSA officially sanctions esports the same way it sanctions traditional sports, with regular seasons, playoffs, and a state championship.

Do I need my own gaming PC or console?

Not necessarily — most schools run matches from a school computer lab or dedicated esports room. Some schools/games allow playing from home. Ask your coach what your school provides.

Is there a cost to join?

Participation is generally free to students; any equipment costs are handled by the school's program, not out of your pocket. Confirm specifics with your coach.

Can I play more than one game?

It depends on your school's roster and PlayVS's scheduling — ask your coach, since match days can overlap.

What if my school doesn't have an esports team yet?

Talk to a teacher you think would sponsor one (CS, media, or tech teachers are common sponsors) — starting a team just means registering the school with PlayVS and getting a roster together.
