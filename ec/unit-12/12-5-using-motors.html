12.5 — Using Motors

🔔 Bell Ringer — Think About It
  1. You already built a DC motor + transistor circuit in Unit 3. What do you remember about why a transistor was necessary?
  2. This lesson introduces a motor controller, which can control both the direction and speed of a motor. Why might controlling direction be a new problem that a simple transistor switch doesn't solve?
  3. What real device can you think of that needs a motor to spin both forward and backward, not just on and off?
  4. How do you think a motor controller might relate to the transistor circuit you already know?

Notes

Look up these definitions:

  • Motor controller
  • Direction control
  • Speed control (PWM review)

Key idea: This lesson introduces motor controllers, which extend the transistor idea from your Unit 3 DC motor lesson — instead of just switching a motor on and off, a motor controller lets you control both direction (forward/backward) and speed. This is directly useful for anything that needs to reverse, like a robot that needs to back up, not just a fan that only ever spins one way.
