6.1 — Product Story: How Nest Was Built

Not a case study — no crime, no Forensics Report. This unit is different: no new hardware, no case lessons, just you designing and building your own original project.

Intro

Ask AI these questions; look over the answers.

  1. What is the Nest Thermostat, and how did its design change across its early versions?
  2. Explain to a middle schooler why a company might release a simpler first version of a product on purpose, rather than waiting until every planned feature is finished.
  3. Create a simple timeline showing Nest's V1, V2, and V3, with what each version added.
  4. What made Nest's thermostat different from a typical, older thermostat that came before it?
  5. Connect Nest's iterative development process to the V1, V2, V3 structure you used in your own RFID access control project last unit — what's similar, and what's different?
  6. What are some real product design competitions or events where students might showcase a project like the one you're about to build this unit?

Notes

Look up these definitions and add them to your living glossary:

  • Product iteration
  • Minimum viable product (MVP)

Nest's story: founded by Tony Fadell (former Apple iPod engineer). V1 replaced clunky old thermostats with a simple, attractive interface. V2 added a learning algorithm that watched a household's adjustment patterns and started predicting temperature automatically. V3 added remote phone control. Google acquired Nest for $3.2 billion in 2014. This is the exact same V1→V2→V3 pattern you lived through in Unit 5 — a real basic working version (MVP) first, then meaningful, specific improvements, not "make it better" in general. This unit, you design that process yourself. SkillsUSA (this course's CTSO, from Unit 1) has real competitive events — Electronics Technology, Engineering Technology/Design — directly relevant to a project like this; Demo Day (6.7) previews what presenting at one might feel like.
