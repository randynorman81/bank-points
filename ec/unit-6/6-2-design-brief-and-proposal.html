6.2 — Design Brief and Proposal

You cannot start Sprint 1 (next lesson) without an approved Design Brief. Get it reviewed and approved in class today.

Intro

Ask AI these questions; look over the answers.

  1. What makes a good design proposal clear and specific, rather than vague? Give me an example of each.
  2. Explain to a middle schooler why engineers write down their plan before building, instead of just starting and figuring it out as they go.
  3. Create a simple checklist someone could use to evaluate whether their own project idea is realistic for the time and components they actually have available.
  4. Why might a team choose to scope down an ambitious idea into something simpler, rather than abandoning it entirely?
  5. Connect today's planning process to something you already did earlier this year that also required a clear plan before building — Unit 5's RFID project, for example.
  6. What questions should a project proposal answer before anyone starts building, to avoid wasted time later?

Notes

Look up these definitions:

  • Design brief
  • Scope
  • Success criteria

This is the first time this year you're proposing something entirely of your own design, with no fixed component list given to you. A strong brief is specific ("a system that detects when someone approaches a door and displays a welcome message on the LCD," not "something with sensors that does cool stuff"), lists exactly which components it needs and why, has checkable success criteria, and keeps realistic scope for the time you have — 4 periods for Sprint 1, 4 for Sprint 2. 2–3 components used well beats 4+ attempted without enough time to make them work together. Don't just re-do the traffic light, alarm, or RFID lock without a genuinely new function or combination.

Assignments

  • 6.2 — Design Brief. Complete the full template: (1) Project Name. (2) What It Does — specific function/problem addressed. (3) Components Needed — every component and why it's necessary. (4) Sprint 1 (MVP) Goal — the simplest version that would still genuinely work. (5) Success Criteria — specific, checkable conditions for success by Demo Day. (6) Possible Sprint 2 Direction — general idea only, not finalized. (7) Realistic Timeline Check — given 4+4 periods, is this achievable? What would you simplify if not? Submit to CodeHS AND get in-class teacher approval today — you cannot proceed to Sprint 1 without it.
