6.6 — Sprint 2 Retrospective

This retrospective looks back across the whole project (both sprints), not just Sprint 2 — and includes real prep for tomorrow's Demo Day. No buffer day between this and presenting, so today's readiness check matters.

Intro

Ask AI these questions; look over the answers.

  1. What makes a live demonstration of a project effective — what should someone presenting focus on, and what should they avoid?
  2. Explain to a middle schooler why explaining your process (not just showing the final result) makes a presentation more interesting and convincing.
  3. Create a simple structure for a short presentation that covers what you built, why, and what you learned.
  4. Why might it be valuable to mention what didn't work, or what you'd improve, during a presentation — rather than only showing the successful parts?
  5. Connect today's presentation prep to a real competitive event format, like a SkillsUSA presentation, where students must explain their work to judges.
  6. What's the difference between a presentation that just shows a finished product working, versus one that demonstrates genuine understanding of how and why it works?

Notes

No new vocabulary today.

Today's retrospective is different from 6.4: look back across the entire project, Design Brief through finished build. Did the finished project match your original vision? Where did it diverge, and was that a good adjustment or a missed goal? A strong Demo Day presentation covers: what you built, why you made the design choices you made, what changed between Sprint 1 and Sprint 2, and one honest limitation you'd improve with more time. Naming a real limitation confidently makes a presentation stronger, not weaker — it shows genuine understanding, not just a working demo. Practice out loud at least once before tomorrow.
