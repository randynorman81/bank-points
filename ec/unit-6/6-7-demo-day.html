6.7 — Demo Day

The unit's culminating event — live presentations, not a standard lesson. Set your project up and ready to demonstrate at the start of class.

Presenting

Your presentation should cover, in order: (1) what your project does — a brief live demonstration; (2) why you made your key design choices; (3) what changed between Sprint 1 and Sprint 2, and why; (4) one honest limitation or thing you'd improve with more time. Keep it within your allotted time.

Individual Reflection

After presentations, write an individual reflection (not a pair submission — this one is just from you):

  • What did you learn from watching your classmates' projects that you didn't get from your own?
  • How does your own project's V1→V2 progression compare to the Nest Thermostat story from Lesson 6.1?
  • What's one thing you're genuinely proud of from this unit, and why?

Assignments

  • 6.7 — Demo Day Individual Reflection. Submit your individual reflection (see above) — the only individual, non-pair submission this unit.
  • Complete Page 2.6 — Unit 6 on your Google Site: your final project photo or video; a connection paragraph (3–4 sentences connecting your own project's development to the Nest Thermostat story); and "what I'd build next." This finishes the page, which has now accumulated content since Lesson 6.1.

What do you actually need to do and turn in?

Present your project live in class, then write and turn in an individual reflection (not a pair submission). Also complete Page 2.6 on your Google Site with your final project media, a Nest connection paragraph, and "what I'd build next."

What do you need to know to complete this?

The 4-part presentation structure from today's Presenting notes, your own finished project, and the Nest Thermostat story from Lesson 6.1.

Where do you turn it in?

The presentation happens live in class; your individual reflection goes in Google Classroom, and the finished Page 2.6 goes on your Google Site.

Solo, partners, or group?

Presenting your project is done with your assigned partner; the written reflection afterward is individual — the only non-pair submission this unit.

How many points is this worth?

100 points — this is the unit's culminating presentation, reflection, and site completion.

Extra credit options

No extra credit is tied to today's lesson.
