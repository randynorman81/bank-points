A 36-week plan (two 18-week semesters). Each unit ends with a Unit Test and a Google Site Check on the same day. Weeks are a target — build days sometimes run long, so expect small shifts.

  • First forensic audit, GPS ankle monitor failures, Smart City Simulator, the Raspberry Pi
  • Unit 1 Study Guide and Test
Weeks 4–6
Unit 2: Arduino Foundations (simulated)
  • TinkerCAD setup, Blink, variables, PWM, timing, circuit fundamentals, interpreted vs. compiled
  • Unit build: Traffic Light
Weeks 7–9

  • The same skills again on a physical Arduino: first real circuit, Blink, variables, PWM, timing
  • Unit build: Traffic Light on real hardware
Weeks 10–14

  • Light, distance, motion, and temperature sensor labs; if/else, loops, functions, debugging
  • V1 and V2 Alarm System build
  • Forensics Reports: Jeep Cherokee hack, Oldsmar water plant, Toyota unintended acceleration
Weeks 15–17
Unit 5: Input Systems
  • Buttons, keypad, PIN entry system, LCD over I2C, keypad + LCD combined, finite state machine
  • Forensics Reports: Target data breach, Stuxnet

Semester 1 review and exam Exam

Course Description

Embedded Computing is a third-level course in the Internet of Things pathway. By the end of the year you will have built real working devices — sensor alarms, keypad systems, an RFID door lock, and a capstone project of your own design. You will also analyze real crimes, disasters, and engineering failures that happened because of embedded technology.

Your Four Ongoing Responsibilities

These are not one-time assignments — they are living records of your work that you maintain all year.

1. Developer Log

A Google Doc you write in during the last 5 minutes of every class. Learning days: what you learned, what surprised you, what you still wonder. Build days: what you built, what worked, what didn’t, what’s next.

Specific, honest entries count. “It kind of worked” does not. Spot-checked at every site check.

2. Build Post

A short, visual entry on your Google Site every time you finish a build: a photo plus 2–3 sentences on what it does and how it works. Each unit page ends with a case-connection paragraph and “what I’d build next.”

Posted the same day you build, not on test day.

3. Forensics Report

A structured 8-section analysis of a real crime, disaster, or engineering failure involving embedded technology — about 13 across the year, on case-study days only. Includes a connected-cases analysis comparing three cases.

Format: Google Doc or Google Slides. Surface-level summaries do not earn full credit.

4. Living Glossary

A running vocabulary table you add to every lesson. Each term gets five columns: the AI’s definition, your own definition, a real-life example, a photo, and your comfort rating (1–5).

Checked at every site check for new terms.

Your Google Site

Your public portfolio for the course. It holds three sections:

  • About Me — your intro and photo, a resume (created in the course and updated at the end of the year), a running Careers in Embedded Computing list (one new career every unit), and your Living Glossary.
  • My Builds — one page per unit with every build, photo, and write-up.
  • Extra Credit — only filled in if you complete Beyond the Build activities.

If your site is unreachable on site-check day, the whole check scores 0. Keep it published.

Submitting Work

  • Assignments are submitted through CodeHS, using the links posted on Mr. Norman’s Google Site.
  • Everything is due Friday at 11:59 pm of the week it is assigned.
  • Accepted formats: Google Doc, Google Slides, PDF upload, or a video, audio, or screencast link.
  • Set sharing to “Anyone with the link can view.” A link that won’t open is returned ungraded.
  • Missing an assignment? Report it on the Late Work page.
