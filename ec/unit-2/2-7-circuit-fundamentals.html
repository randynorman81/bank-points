2.7 — Circuit Fundamentals: Source, Sink, Pull-Up, Ohm's Law

🔔 Bell Ringer — Think About It
  1. A floating pin doesn't just stay silently at 0 — it can pick up totally random noise. Why do you think an "unset" wire behaves unpredictably instead of just defaulting to off?
  2. The Arduino Uno's pins can only safely handle about 40mA before something gets damaged. Why do you think the pin has a hard limit like that instead of being built to handle whatever current a circuit throws at it?
  3. Ohm's Law lets you calculate the exact resistor a circuit needs before you ever touch a real component. Why does knowing the math matter here, when you could just try a few resistors and see which one works?
  4. A pull-up resistor exists purely to give an "unset" pin a predictable default state. Can you think of a situation in everyday life where having a clear, predictable default matters just as much as it does here?

Notes

Look up these definitions:

  • Source current
  • Sink current
  • Pull-up resistor
  • Floating pin
  • Ohm's Law

Key idea: a pin sources current when it pushes current out (digitalWrite HIGH), and sinks current when current flows into it. A button wired with no defined HIGH/LOW when unpressed is floating — it picks up random noise and gives unreliable readings. A pull-up resistor fixes this by connecting the pin to 5V by default (the Uno has a built-in one via pinMode(pin, INPUT_PULLUP)). Ohm's Law: V = IR (voltage = current × resistance) — rearrange to R = V/I to find the resistor an LED circuit needs. The Uno's pins max out around 40mA — exceeding that can damage the pin or board, which is exactly why every LED gets a resistor.
