2.2 — First Circuit in the Circuit Editor

🔔 Bell Ringer — Think About It
  1. An LED wired backwards in real life sometimes just won't light up, but a different part wired backwards could get damaged or even start a small fire. Why do you think engineers simulate a circuit first instead of just building it and seeing what happens?
  2. A breadboard's rows are secretly wired together internally, even though nothing is visibly connecting them. Why do you think breadboards are designed this way instead of just being a blank grid of holes?
  3. If simulation mistakes are free but real hardware mistakes can cost money or get someone hurt, should every engineer be required to simulate before touching real components, or are there times skipping straight to hardware makes sense?
  4. Polarity means direction matters — plug something in backwards and it fails instead of just working oddly. Can you think of another everyday object, not electrical, where "backwards" completely breaks something instead of just looking different?
