Notes

Look up these definitions and add them to your living glossary:

  • Breadboard
  • Schematic
  • Circuit simulation
  • Polarity

Key idea: a breadboard's rows are electrically connected internally — components plugged into the same row are connected to each other, and the two outer rows on each side are power rails (one positive, one ground). An LED has polarity: the longer leg is positive (anode), the shorter leg is negative (cathode) — wired backwards, it won't light up. Polarity matters for every LED circuit for the rest of the year. Engineers simulate before building with real hardware because mistakes are free in simulation and no real component can get damaged.
