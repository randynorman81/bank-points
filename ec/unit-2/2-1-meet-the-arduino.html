2.1 — Meet the Arduino

🔔 Bell Ringer — Think About It
  1. Arduino's hardware designs are open source — anyone can legally copy and sell their own version of the board. Why would a company give away the exact blueprint a competitor could use to undercut them, and how might that actually help Arduino instead of hurting it?
  2. The Arduino board and the Arduino IDE are two completely separate things that just share a name. Why do you think keeping the "brain" (code) and the "body" (hardware) separate is useful for engineers, instead of building one that only works with the other?
  3. If you were choosing hardware for a real project, like a small weather station, what would make you pick an Arduino Uno over a Raspberry Pi, or the other way around?
  4. A digital pin only knows HIGH or LOW, on or off, nothing in between. Why would engineers ever want a pin that limited, when an analog pin can read a whole range of values?

Directions

  1. Pick a side: Team Analog or Team Digital.
  2. Build your case using 3 real-world fields: weather, music, and video.
  3. Prepare your Arduino "Exhibit A" — digitalRead() vs. analogRead().
  4. Present your case in class, then submit it in Google Classroom.

Assignments

  • 2.1 — Analog vs. Digital. You and your partner will research the concepts of ANALOG and DIGITAL. One of you argues that Analog is better. The other argues that Digital is better. You'll use three topics as your evidence: weather, music, and video.

    Step-by-Step Directions

    1. Pick your side with your partner. One of you argues Analog. The other argues Digital. Decide this together before you start working, and don't switch once you've decided.
    2. Choose your products (listed below):
      TopicAnalog ExampleDigital Example
      WeatherA mercury thermometerA digital weather app/sensor
      MusicVinyl record or cassette tapeCD or streaming app
      VideoVHS tapeBlu-ray or Netflix
    3. Answer the questions:
      • 3–5 sentences explaining what makes it analog or digital (general concept)
      • 3–5 sentences explaining why it is better or worse than its counterpart (persuade)
      • 3–5 sentences explaining what analog or digital is to a 4th grader (basic explain)
      • 3–5 sentences explaining it to someone with a degree in computer science (advanced explain)
      • 2–5 sentences explaining what AnalogRead() does on the Arduino (even if you chose digital)
      • 2–5 sentences explaining what DigitalRead() does on the Arduino (even if you chose analog)
      • 2–5 sentences explaining which one you think would be more useful on the Arduino (AnalogRead() or DigitalRead()?)
    4. Write your answers in whatever format you pick (see "Format Options").
