2.6 — Timing and Sequences

Two-period lesson, new wiring — three LEDs today (red, yellow, green). This directly sets up your Unit 2 build in Lesson 2.9.

Intro

Ask AI these questions; look over the answers.

  1. How does a real traffic light control the timing and order of red, yellow, and green lights? Walk me through the sequence.
  2. Explain to a middle schooler why a computer program can only do one thing at a time inside delay(), even if it looks like things are happening "at once."
  3. Create a simple timeline or flowchart showing a 3-LED sequence — which LED is on at which point in time, in order.
  4. What is the limitation of using delay() in a program? What can't happen while delay() is running?
  5. Connect sequencing lights in a specific order to a real-world device or system I might be familiar with — where else does timing and order matter this much?
  6. What happens in a real traffic intersection if the timing sequence has a bug — what are the actual real-world consequences?

Notes

Look up these definitions and add them to your living glossary:

  • Sequence
  • Blocking code
  • State
  • Timing logic

Key idea: delay() pauses your entire program — nothing else can run while it's counting down. This is called blocking code, and it's fine for everything in this unit, but it becomes a real limitation starting in Unit 3 (more on that with interrupts). Wiring three LEDs: each needs its own resistor and its own digital pin, but they can all share one ground connection back to GND. A sequence is just digitalWrite() + delay() repeated in a deliberate order across multiple pins.
