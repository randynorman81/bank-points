PWM: Controlling Brightness

Everything you need for the 2.5 assignment: what PWM really is, why your LED moves to pin 9, how analogWrite() works, and how to explain PWM in your two cartoons.

01

What PWM Actually Is

Intro + Part 2

A digital pin has no "halfway" setting. It is fully on (5V) or fully off (0V), period. So how do you make an LED look half as bright? You cheat: flip it on and off hundreds of times a second, faster than your eyes can follow, and let your brain average it out.

Pulse Width Modulation

Pulse: each quick burst of "on." Width: how long each burst lasts. Modulation: changing it. So PWM means changing how long the pin stays on in each quick cycle.

Duty cycle

The percent of each cycle the pin is on. 0% = always off. 100% = always on. 50% = on half the time, which your eyes see as about half brightness.

Try it: analogWrite value

Slow motion shows what's really happening: the LED is only ever fully on or fully off. At normal speed, it switches about 490 times a second on pin 9.

02

Why Your LED Moves to Pin 9

Part 1 Step 1

Only some pins have the hardware to do the fast switching. On the Uno they're marked with a little squiggle (~) next to the number. Your LED has been on pin 8 since 2.3, and pin 8 isn't one of them. Click a pin to check it.

Before you write any code, move your LED's wire from pin 8 to pin 9. Any ~ pin would work, but pin 9 matches the example. On a pin without ~, analogWrite() can't dim: the LED just snaps fully on or fully off.
03

analogWrite(): Part 1 Code

Part 1 Steps 2–7

analogWrite() looks like digitalWrite(), but instead of HIGH or LOW you give it a number from 0 to 255. 0 means off, 255 means full brightness, and 128 is about half.

int ledPin = 9; // changed from 8: pin 9 is a PWM (~) pin void setup() { pinMode(ledPin, OUTPUT); } void loop() { analogWrite(ledPin, 50); // dim delay(1000); analogWrite(ledPin, 150); // medium delay(1000); analogWrite(ledPin, 255); // full brightness delay(1000); }

Preview your own three numbers (Step 4)

Explaining each line (Step 5): for every line, say what it tells the Arduino to do and why it's there. Example: delay(1000); pauses for 1000 milliseconds so you have time to see that brightness level before the next one.
04

Planning Your Two Cartoons

Part 2

Same idea, two audiences. A good cartoon picks one clear comparison and shows it happening, instead of filling the panels with text.

Middle schooler (3 cells)

  • Comparisons they know: flicking a light switch super fast, a flipbook, a fan blade that looks solid when it spins.
  • Show the three ideas: the light can only be on or off, it switches too fast to see, and more "on" time looks brighter.
  • Simple words. Define PWM once, in plain language.

Computer science college student

  • Use the real terms: duty cycle, frequency (about 490 Hz on pin 9), 8-bit resolution (0–255, so 256 levels).
  • Average voltage: 50% duty cycle averages to about 2.5V.
  • Why it looks smooth: the switching is faster than the eye's persistence of vision.
  • It's still a digital signal, not true analog output, even though the function is called analogWrite().
Assignment requirements: any program or hand-drawn, in color, interesting, correct, and complete.
