2.3 — Your First Sketch: Blink

Two-period lesson. Day 1: wire your first real circuit and get Blink running. Day 2: finish, modify the timing, and turn in.

Intro

Ask AI these questions; look over the answers.

  1. What is the "Blink" sketch in Arduino and why is it considered the standard first program for beginners?
  2. Explain to a middle schooler what setup() and loop() do in an Arduino program, using an analogy from everyday life.
  3. Create a simple flowchart showing what happens step by step when an Arduino runs a Blink program — from power on to the LED blinking repeatedly.
  4. What is the difference between digitalWrite() and just writing a regular line of code in a normal programming language? Why does this command exist specifically for hardware?
  5. Connect uploading your first program to a real device to a movie or TV show I might know — where has a character brought something to life for the first time?
  6. What is the very first program most programmers write when learning any new language or platform, and why is starting simple considered good practice?

Notes

Look up these definitions:

  • Sketch
  • setup()
  • loop()
  • digitalWrite()

Key idea: every Arduino sketch has exactly two required functions — setup() runs once when the board powers on (use it to configure pins with pinMode()), and loop() runs forever after that. digitalWrite(pin, HIGH) turns a pin on (5V); digitalWrite(pin, LOW) turns it off (0V). delay(1000) pauses the program for 1000 milliseconds — without it, the blink would happen too fast to see. Common first-upload problems: wrong board or port selected in the IDE, missing semicolons/unmatched braces, or an LED wired backwards.

Assignments

  • 2.3 — Blink Sketch: Real Hardware. Wire an LED circuit on your breadboard (same layout as your TinkerCAD circuit, now with real hardware), upload the Blink sketch, and get it working. Then modify the timing so it's no longer the default 1000ms — make it blink faster, slower, or in an uneven pattern. Annotate every line of your code with a comment. Submit your annotated code, a photo of your working circuit, and a brief reflection on one problem you debugged and how you fixed it.
