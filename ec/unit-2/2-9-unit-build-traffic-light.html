2.9 — Unit Build: Traffic Light

🔔 Bell Ringer — Think About It
  1. This is the first project this unit where using AI to write your actual solution defeats the point, even though using it to understand an error is fine. What's the real difference between those two uses, and why does that line matter for what you're actually being graded on?
  2. You went from a single simulated LED, to timed sequences, to a full three-LED traffic light you built in TinkerCAD without step-by-step instructions. Which of those stages taught you the most, and why?
  3. A real embedded engineer never gets a lesson page walking them through their own project. If you got stuck today with no directions to fall back on, what's the first thing you'd actually try before asking for help?
  4. Your traffic light works even though nobody is grading whether the timing is "realistic" down to the second. Why might building something that just works still not be good enough for a real engineer shipping a product?

Notes

No new vocabulary today.

What you're building: a working traffic light using red, yellow, and green simulated LEDs in TinkerCAD, controlled by a program you write independently, combining everything from this unit — digital output (2.3), variables (2.4), timing/sequencing (2.6), and safe circuit wiring (2.7). Requirements: all three LEDs wired correctly with resistors; realistic, differentiated timing (not identical delays); at least one variable used somewhere in your code; every line commented; a fully working, debugged final simulation. This is not a day to ask AI to generate your code — using it to understand a specific error is fine, having it write your solution defeats the point.
