2.9 — Unit Build: Traffic Light

Independent build — two periods. This is the first fully independent build of the year: minimal help, maximum application of what you've already learned this unit.

Extra Credit Quiz

Today we'll take the 2.9 Quiz — 10 questions for extra credit. The first 5 cover earlier material; the last 5 cover this unit.

Notes

No new vocabulary today.

What you're building: a working traffic light using red, yellow, and green LEDs, controlled by a program you write independently, combining everything from this unit — digital output (2.3), variables (2.4), timing/sequencing (2.6), and safe circuit wiring (2.7). Requirements: all three LEDs wired correctly with resistors; realistic, differentiated timing (not identical delays); at least one variable used somewhere in your code; every line commented; a fully working, debugged final product. This is not a day to ask AI to generate your code — using it to understand a specific error is fine, having it write your solution defeats the point.

Assignments

  • 2.9 — Unit Build: Traffic Light. Build a working traffic light: wire your three LEDs (you may reuse your Lesson 2.6 wiring if it's intact), plan your program before writing it, then write, upload, and debug it independently. Submit your fully annotated code (using at least one variable), a photo of your finished, working build, and a brief reflection on your build process, including one specific problem you debugged on your own.
  • Add to Page 2.2 — Unit 2: Arduino Foundations on your Google Site: your traffic light build photo; 2–3 sentences on what it does and how it works; a connection paragraph (Unit 2 has no case study — instead reflect on how you moved from simulation to prototype to a finished device, same as a real embedded engineer); and one thing you'd build next if you had more time.
