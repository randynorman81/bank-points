5.6 — V2: Servo Door Lock

Two-period lesson. New hardware: the servo motor — the first motor of the year. RC522 reading and whitelist logic are pure review from 5.3–5.4.

Intro

Ask AI these questions; look over the answers.

  1. How does a servo motor achieve precise position control, as opposed to a regular motor that just spins continuously?
  2. Explain to a middle schooler how PWM, which we used to control LED brightness back in Unit 2, can also be used to control a servo's exact angle.
  3. Create a simple diagram showing the relationship between a PWM signal's duty cycle and the resulting servo angle.
  4. Why do servos have a limited rotation range (usually around 180 degrees) instead of spinning continuously like a regular motor?
  5. Connect today's servo-controlled lock to a real product you might recognize that uses a servo for precise positioning.
  6. Does adding a physical lock to an access control system fix a vulnerability like UID cloning, or does it add something different on top of an existing weakness?

Notes

Look up these definitions and add them to your living glossary:

  • Servo motor
  • Pulse width
  • Position control
  • Mechanical actuation

Key idea: a servo moves to and holds a specific angle (usually within ~180°) rather than spinning continuously. This uses the exact same PWM technique from Lesson 2.5 — back then duty cycle controlled brightness, today pulse width tells the servo what angle to move to. A servo has 3 wires (power, ground, signal) — signal goes to a PWM-capable pin, and a Servo library handles the angle math for you.

Be honest with yourself here: adding a servo lock is a real functional improvement (a physical mechanism now responds to the access decision), but it does not fix the UID-cloning vulnerability from 5.2/5.5 — a cloned card would still unlock this exact door. V2 improves functionality, not security.
