5.1 — What Is RFID?

No hardware today — RC522 wiring (3.3V only, never 5V!) begins next lesson.

Intro

Ask AI these questions; look over the answers.

  1. What is RFID, and how does a card or tag transmit data to a reader without any physical contact or even a battery?
  2. Explain to a middle schooler the difference between RFID and NFC — they're related, but not the same thing.
  3. Create a simple diagram showing how an RFID reader and an RFID card communicate using radio waves.
  4. What is a UID in the context of RFID, and why might relying only on a UID for security be a weakness?
  5. Connect RFID to a real product or system you personally use — a hotel key, a building badge, a transit pass, a toll tag.
  6. What are some real industries or use cases for RFID beyond access control — for example, in retail, shipping, or even pets?

Notes

Look up these definitions:

  • RFID (Radio-Frequency Identification)
  • NFC (Near-Field Communication)
  • UID (Unique Identifier)
  • Authentication

Key idea — the most important concept of this whole unit: most RFID cards are passive (no battery) — the reader's own radio signal briefly powers the card's chip just long enough for it to respond. Every card has a UID, and a basic system just checks whether a scanned UID is on an authorized list. The critical weakness: a UID is just data broadcast over radio — with inexpensive equipment, it can be captured and cloned onto another card that behaves identically to any system checking only the UID. This single weakness is why this unit's entire case cluster and V1→V2→V3 build sequence exist.
