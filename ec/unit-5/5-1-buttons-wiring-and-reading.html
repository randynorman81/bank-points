5.1 — Buttons: Wiring and Reading

🔔 Bell Ringer — Think About It
  1. Reading two buttons "independently" sounds simple, but today's notes call this direct prep for the keypad, which is really just many buttons read more efficiently. Why might mastering something small and boring now save you from a much harder debugging problem later?
  2. A Scrum standup is supposed to take seconds per pair, not minutes. Why would a team deliberately keep a daily check-in short instead of using that time to explain everything in detail?
  3. Your combined-behavior design today, like "both LEDs blink only when both buttons are pressed," uses &&. Can you think of a real device or app that requires two separate confirmations before it does something risky, and why require both instead of just one?
  4. This unit's whole sprint goal is a working keypad + LCD PIN system. Why might a team name and plan a two-week goal in advance instead of just building things as ideas come up?

Directions

  1. Wire two buttons and two LEDs, each pair independent.
  2. Confirm each button controls its own LED.
  3. Design and build a combined behavior using &&.
  4. Test all four press combinations.
  5. Submit your code and photo in Google Classroom.

The wiring here is review from 2.7/3.4 — move through it quickly. What's genuinely new: reading two buttons independently, the && operator, and your first formal Scrum standup.

Notes

Look up these definitions:

  • Scrum
  • Sprint
  • Standup
  • Backlog

Starting today, this course uses real Scrum vocabulary: a sprint is a focused build period with a goal (this unit's sprint goal: a working keypad + LCD PIN entry system, built across 5.3–5.6); standup is a brief check-in at the start of build periods — three questions only: what did I do last time, what am I doing today, any blockers? Keep it to seconds per pair, not minutes. A backlog is planned-but-not-yet-built features (used in 5.9). For today's build: each button needs its own pinMode() and digitalRead() — track and respond to each independently. This directly preps you for the keypad in 5.3, which is really just many buttons read more efficiently.

Assignments

  • 5.1 — Buttons: Wiring and Reading. Wire two buttons (INPUT_PULLUP, review) and two LEDs, each pair independent. Once each button correctly controls its own LED, design and build a combined behavior of your own — for example, both LEDs blink together only when both buttons are pressed simultaneously. Test all four combinations: neither pressed, only 1, only 2, both. Submit your annotated code and a photo of your working circuit.
