5.9 — Motor Types: Conceptual Overview

No hardware today — conceptual only. Full stepper/brushless motor builds are a Year 2 topic.

Intro

  1. What is a stepper motor, and how does it move in precise, discrete steps rather than spinning smoothly like a regular motor?

  4. Why might a 3D printer use a stepper motor instead of a regular motor or a servo?

Notes

Look up these definitions and add them to your living glossary:

  • Stepper motor
  • Brushed DC motor
  • Brushless motor
  • Continuous rotation
  • Motor selection

Key idea: stepper motors move in precise, discrete steps and can rotate continuously in either direction — great for 3D printers/CNC where exact positioning matters. Brushed DC motors use physical contacts to deliver current — simple, cheap, common in toys, but the brushes wear out. Brushless motors replace brushes with electronic switching — more efficient and longer-lasting, common in drones and EVs. Your servo (5.6) is built specifically for position-holding within a limited range — the right tool for a lock needing exactly two held positions. A stepper would be overkill; a brushless motor (built for continuous rotation) would be the wrong tool entirely. Motor selection is choosing the right type for the actual task, not just whatever's available.
