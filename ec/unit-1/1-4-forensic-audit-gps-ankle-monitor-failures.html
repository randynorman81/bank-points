1.4 — Take a Stand: Who's Responsible for GPS Ankle Monitor Failures?

🔔 Bell Ringer — Think About It
  1. A GPS ankle monitor can be wrong for a few minutes and put an innocent person back in jail. Should a device with that much power over someone's freedom be allowed to have any known glitches at all — or is "no technology is perfect" an acceptable excuse here?
  2. Three different companies touch this kind of system: the hardware maker, the monitoring company, and the government agency. Before today's research, which one would you guess usually gets blamed the most when something like this happens — and is that actually fair?
  3. A monitoring company's own rules might say to double-check a reading before acting on it, but an employee skips that step under pressure. Is a company responsible for its rules being ignored, or only for the rules it wrote?
  4. If wearing a location tracker is a condition of someone's freedom, how much accuracy should the law require before that tracker is allowed to be used as evidence against a real person?

Directions

  1. Open today's Activity page (linked below) for the scenario, the three parties, and how the room debate works.
  2. Decide which party you believe is most responsible.
  3. Write a half-page response giving 3 reasons why you chose that side.
  4. Cite at least one specific fact from today's research for each reason.
  5. If you switched spots during the discussion, explain what changed your mind.
  6. Submit your reflection in Google Classroom.
