1.2 — Intro to Forensic Auditing

🔔 Bell Ringer — Think About It
  1. A forensic audit's job is to figure out exactly what went wrong and who's responsible — not to guess or assume. Why is the difference between "who looks guilty" and "who the evidence actually proves is responsible" so important when the stakes are someone's safety?
  2. If a piece of technology fails and hurts someone, blame could land on the engineer who wrote the code, the company that sold it, or the person who used it. Why might a real investigation need to consider all three before deciding?
  3. Could a system be completely "working as designed" and still be responsible for causing harm? What would that even mean?
  4. Why do you think forensic auditors are trained to follow the facts wherever they lead, even if the conclusion is unpopular or points at the company paying for the audit?
