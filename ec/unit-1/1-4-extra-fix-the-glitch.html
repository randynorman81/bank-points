The pitch

You just got hired as an embedded systems engineer at Meridian Trace Technologies — yes, the company from earlier that knew about the GPS drift glitch and shipped it anyway. Your new boss is done being sued. Your job: walk into Friday's engineering review with an actual fix, not another excuse.

You don't get to just say "make the GPS better." You have to propose one hardware change and one software/logic change, and explain exactly how each one would have stopped today's wrongful arrest from happening.

Intro

Ask AI these questions; look over the answers — these are more technical than this morning's, on purpose.

  1. What causes GPS "drift" or multipath error, and why does it happen more in some locations (cities, forests, indoors) than others?
  2. What is sensor fusion, and how do phones and cars combine GPS with other signals (cell tower triangulation, Wi-Fi positioning, accelerometer data) to get a more reliable location?
  3. What is a confidence threshold in a sensor system, and how could requiring "two bad readings in a row" instead of one prevent a false alarm?
  4. What's a watchdog timer or sanity-check logic in embedded systems, and how might one apply to a device that suddenly reports an impossible jump in location?
  5. Explain to a middle schooler why "just make the sensor perfect" isn't a realistic engineering fix, and why systems are usually designed to expect errors instead.

Today's Activity — Fix the Glitch

Working solo or with a partner, design your fix using the form below. It needs:

  • One hardware fix — something physically different about the monitor itself (example categories to research, not answers: a second positioning method as backup, a better antenna, an onboard sanity-check chip — you don't have to use these).
  • One software/logic fix — something different about how the device or GuardLine's system decides a reading is real (example categories: requiring confirmation pings, comparing against the person's recent movement history, flagging — not auto-escalating — a single suspicious reading).
  • A one-line "before vs. after" for each fix: what exactly would have happened differently in this morning's scenario if your fix had already shipped.
  • A quick sketch of your hardware fix using the drawing pad below.

Constraint that makes this harder (and more realistic): your fix can't make the monitor take longer than 60 seconds to report a real zone violation. A fix that's so cautious it lets someone actually run also fails — Meridian's other customer is public safety, not just the wrongly accused.

Your proposal

Pitch it

Two minutes, no slides required. To the "engineering review board" (the class): what's broken, what you're changing, and why your fix beats just "add more testing." Best pitches get bonus points for a fix nobody else thought of.
