8.3 — Sprint 1 Retrospective

Same three-question format as every retrospective this year, now scaled to a 6-period capstone sprint. There's no Sprint 3 after this — whatever you plan today is your last chance to improve this project.

Intro

Ask AI these questions; look over the answers.

  1. Why might a retrospective after a 6-period sprint require more depth than one after a shorter build?
  2. Explain to a middle schooler why honestly assessing a big, important project is sometimes harder than assessing a smaller one — what makes honesty harder when more is at stake?
  3. Create a template for planning a final phase of a capstone project, given that there's no opportunity to build a third version afterward.
  4. How should a team prioritize a final improvement when there's no more time after this one to make further changes?
  5. Connect today's reflection to your own Unit 6 retrospective from earlier this year — what's different about reflecting on a capstone versus a smaller unit project?
  6. Why might it be especially important to be honest about real limitations in a capstone retrospective specifically, compared to a lower-stakes assignment?

Notes

No new vocabulary today.

Same three questions, greater depth expected: what went well (specific moments/decisions across a genuinely long, complex sprint)? What didn't go well (honest, real friction across 6 full periods)? What would you do differently? Sprint 2 is the final improvement this project will receive in this course — if Sprint 1's core goal isn't fully done, Sprint 2 should finish it AND add something genuinely new.

Assignments

  • 8.3 — Sprint 1 Retrospective + Sprint 2 Plan. Part 1: a genuine, specific retrospective on Sprint 1, with depth appropriate to a 6-period capstone sprint. Part 2: your Sprint 2 plan — the genuinely new element you'll add, what (if anything) still needs finishing, and what success looks like, knowing this is your last chance to improve the project. Format: Doc or Slides.
