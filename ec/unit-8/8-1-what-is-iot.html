8.1 — What Is IoT? + AI in IoT Deep Dive

🔔 Bell Ringer — Think About It
  1. Every project you've built this year — traffic light, alarm, PIN entry, RFID lock — was fully functional but never connected to a network. What's one thing your RFID lock could do if it were suddenly IoT-connected that it absolutely cannot do right now?
  2. A device ecosystem means everything works together conveniently, but it also means one vulnerability in the shared service can affect every connected device at once. Would you rather own five separate simple devices, or one connected smart-home system where they all talk to each other?
  3. If IoT means physical devices connected to a network, is your school's badge-scanning door system IoT? What about a traffic light that reports its own malfunction to the city? Where exactly is the line?
  4. Think about how many "smart" devices are already in your own house — TV, thermostat, doorbell, speaker. Did you ever actually decide you wanted a connected version of that device, or did it just come that way by default?

Notes

Look up these definitions:

  • IoT (Internet of Things)
  • Smart device
  • Device ecosystem
  • Data exchange
  • Connectivity

Key idea: IoT means physical, embedded devices connected to a network and each other — not just "anything involving computers." Your traffic light (Unit 2), alarm (Unit 4), PIN entry system (Unit 5), and RFID lock (Unit 6) were all standalone embedded systems — fully functional but not IoT, because none had connectivity. Lesson 8.6's Arduino Cloud lab will be this course's first genuinely connected build. Device ecosystems create convenience (everything works together) but also risk (one vulnerability in the central service can affect every connected device at once) — this sets up 8.5 and 8.7's cases directly.
