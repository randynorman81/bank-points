8.1 — Capstone Proposal

The final, largest project of the course. You cannot start Sprint 1 without an approved proposal by the end of Day 2 — there's no buffer unit after this one to recover lost time.

Extra Credit Quiz

Today we'll take the 8.1 Quiz — 10 questions for extra credit. The first 5 cover earlier material; the last 5 cover this unit.

Notes

Look up these definitions:

  • Capstone project
  • Integration breadth

This capstone should reflect the full breadth of what you've learned all year — not just repeat Unit 6's scope at greater length. A strong proposal draws on multiple units: sensors (Unit 3), input systems (Unit 4), RFID/motors (Unit 5), even networking/cloud concepts (Unit 7) if you choose. 12 total sprint periods (6+6) is double Unit 6's 8 — more room for ambition, but 3–4 components thoughtfully integrated still beats 6+ attempted without enough time. If your instinct is a slightly bigger version of your Unit 6 project, ask: what unit's skills haven't I used independently yet?

Capstone Proposal Template

  1. Project Name
  2. What It Does — a clear, specific description of your project's function.
  3. Components Needed — list every component, and which unit you learned it in.
  4. Integration Breadth Check — how many different units' skills does this genuinely combine? List them.
  5. Sprint 1 Goal — the first major milestone, achievable within 6 periods.
  6. Sprint 2 Direction (general idea only) — a likely meaningful improvement, refined after Sprint 1's retrospective.
  7. Success Criteria — specific, checkable conditions for Sprint 1 and the overall finished capstone.
  8. Realistic Timeline Check — given 6+6 periods, is this achievable? What would you simplify if not?
  9. Comparison to Your Unit 6 Project — 2–3 sentences on how this is genuinely more ambitious.

Assignments

  • 8.1 — Capstone Proposal. Complete the full template above. Submit to CodeHS AND get in-class teacher approval by the end of Day 2 — you cannot start Sprint 1 without it.
