3.8 — Case: Oldsmar Water Plant

Intro

Ask AI these questions; look over the answers.

  1. What happened at the Oldsmar, Florida water treatment plant in 2021? What did the hacker actually change?
  2. Explain to a middle schooler what sodium hydroxide is used for in water treatment, and why changing its level dramatically would be dangerous.
  3. Create a simple timeline showing what happened, in order, from the hacker gaining access to the operator catching and reversing the change.
  4. Why didn't the system automatically reject or flag a change this extreme? What kind of safeguard was missing?
  5. Connect this case to something else you've learned about this year — what's similar between this and an earlier case we studied?
  6. What real changes did water treatment facilities make to their security after this incident became public?

Notes

Look up these definitions:

  • Industrial control system (ICS)
  • Setpoint
  • Critical infrastructure
  • Remote monitoring

Background: in February 2021, a hacker remotely accessed the industrial control system at a Florida water treatment plant and changed the sodium hydroxide setpoint from 111 parts per million to 11,100 — roughly a 100× increase, potentially lethal. A plant operator happened to be watching the screen and reversed it before it reached the water supply. The system accepted the change without flagging it as suspicious, despite being wildly outside any normal range — the same missing input-validation theme as the Jeep Cherokee case, but at the scale of an entire community's critical infrastructure. Human attention caught this, not an automated safeguard.
