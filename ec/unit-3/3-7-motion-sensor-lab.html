3.7 — Motion Sensor Lab

Two-period lesson, new sensor: the PIR (Passive Infrared) motion sensor, paired with your buzzer from 3.5.

Intro

Ask AI these questions; look over the answers.

  1. What is a PIR motion sensor, and what does "passive infrared" actually mean? Explain it to a middle schooler.
  2. How is a PIR motion sensor different from the ultrasonic distance sensor we used a few lessons ago? What is each one actually detecting?
  3. Create a simple diagram showing how a PIR sensor detects a change in infrared (heat) energy when something warm moves through its field of view.
  4. Why might a PIR motion sensor need a brief warm-up period after it's first powered on before it gives reliable readings?
  5. Connect today's motion sensor to a real security or home automation product I might recognize.
  6. Could a PIR motion sensor be tricked or fail to detect something that's actually moving? What conditions might cause that?

Notes

Look up these definitions:

  • PIR sensor (Passive Infrared)
  • Passive sensor
  • Field of view

Key idea: a PIR sensor detects changes in infrared (heat) radiation in its field of view — it's passive because it only receives existing energy, unlike the active HC-SR04 which sends out a sound pulse. Output is a simple digital HIGH (motion) / LOW (no motion), read with a single digitalRead(). Many PIR sensors need 30–60 seconds to warm up after power-on before readings stabilize — that's normal, not a wiring problem. Field of view is a real physical limit: motion outside the sensor's cone-shaped range won't be detected, even if it's close.

Assignments

  • 3.7 — Motion Sensor Lab. Wire the PIR sensor (VCC→5V, GND→GND, OUT to a digital pin), give it its warm-up time, then read and print its output with digitalRead(). Test by moving in and out of its field of view. Then wire your buzzer and add if/else logic so it sounds when motion is detected. Optionally add a minimum on-duration so it doesn't flicker at the edge of detection. Submit your annotated code and a photo or short video of the working alert system.
