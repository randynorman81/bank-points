3.12 — Case: Toyota Unintended Acceleration

🔔 Bell Ringer — Think About It
  1. A fully mechanical throttle physically can't get "confused" by bad code, but it also can't do anything clever. Once a car's throttle is controlled by software instead of a direct cable, what new kind of failure becomes possible that never existed before?
  2. "Fail-safe logic" means a system defaults to the safe option when something goes wrong. What would "safe" even mean for a car's accelerator, and who decides that when the driver, the software, and physics might all disagree in the same instant?
  3. A single bad global variable can affect code in many different places at once, which makes it hard to trace. Why might a small, hard-to-find software bug be just as dangerous in a two-ton vehicle as a major mechanical failure?
  4. Should a driver ever be expected to physically fight a car's own software for control of something as basic as slowing down? What should a car be required to guarantee no matter what its code is doing?
