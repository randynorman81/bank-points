Buzzer: Tones and Melodies

Everything you need for the 3.2 assignment: how a buzzer makes sound, the note frequencies, tone() and noTone(), arrays, for loops, and a song that starts with a button. Turn your sound on.

01

Sound Is Vibration

Every sound you've ever heard is air shaking. A piezo buzzer shakes when its pin switches on and off very fast, and the faster it switches, the higher the note. Click a key to hear exactly the frequency your Arduino will play.

Frequency is measured in hertz (Hz): times per second. A (440 Hz) means the buzzer vibrates 440 times every second. Notice high C (523) is about double low C (262): an octave up doubles the frequency.

tone(pin, frequency)

Starts a sound at that pitch and keeps it going until something stops it or changes it.

Add a third number, tone(pin, frequency, duration), and it plays for that many milliseconds and stops on its own.

noTone(pin)

Stops the sound. Without it, the last note keeps buzzing.

03

Arrays and the Scale

Part 2

Eight notes could mean eight tone() lines. Or you could put all eight numbers in one list, give the list a name, and let a loop walk through it. That list is an array.

const int BUZZER = 8; int scale[] = {262, 294, 330, 349, 392, 440, 494, 523}; void loop() { for (int i = 0; i < 8; i++) { // i = 0, 1, 2 ... 7 tone(BUZZER, scale[i]); // play the item at position i delay(400); } noTone(BUZZER); delay(2000); }

Watch the loop walk the array

Press play to step through scale[0] to scale[7].

Arrays count from 0

The first item is scale[0], not scale[1]. An array of 8 items has positions 0 through 7. That's why the loop runs while i < 8.

Leave setup() empty for now

Part 2 doesn't need anything in setup(). Turn your computer's sound on, click Start Simulation, and listen.

04

Your Own Melody, Started by a Button

Part 3

A song is two lists: which note, and how long. Keep them side by side in two arrays of the same length, and item 0 of each belongs together, item 1 belongs together, and so on.

The two arrays

  • notes: the frequency of each note, from the table above.
  • beats: how long each note lasts in milliseconds, like 400 or 800.
  • They must be the same length. Your song needs at least 8 notes and at least 2 different note lengths.

The pieces of loop()

  • pinMode(2, INPUT_PULLUP); goes in setup().
  • if (digitalRead(2) == LOW) { means "if the button is pressed."
  • Inside it, you write the for loop that plays each note with tone(BUZZER, notes[i], beats[i]); and waits with delay(beats[i] + 50);
  • After the for loop, still inside the if: noTone(BUZZER);

Try your song before you code it

Click keys to add notes, pick a length for each, then play it back. This is only for planning; you still write the arrays and the loop yourself.

05

Why the Delay Matters

Reflections 1 + 2

tone() doesn't wait. It starts the sound and instantly moves on to the next line. So what decides how long you actually hear each note?

Play the scale two ways and compare. Then think about what the Arduino does in the time between the two tone() calls.

Arrays vs. a line per note (Reflection 2): picture a 40-note song written as 80 lines of tone() and delay(). Now picture changing the tempo, or fixing one wrong note. Where would you make those changes each way?
