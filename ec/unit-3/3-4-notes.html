01

A Pin Is Too Weak for a Motor

Reflection 1

An LED sips current. A motor gulps it. Plug a motor straight into a pin and you're asking a drinking straw to fill a swimming pool, and the straw (your pin) is what gets damaged.

02

The Transistor: an Electronic Switch

Reflection 2

You flip a light switch with one finger, and the switch lets a big current reach the ceiling light. A transistor is that switch, except the "finger" is a tiny signal from your Arduino pin. Click the pin to send the signal.

LegJob
BaseThe switch. Your Arduino pin connects here, through a resistor.
CollectorConnects to the motor. Current flows in here.
EmitterConnects to GND. Current flows out here.
Leg order changes from part to part. In Tinkercad, hover over each leg of your transistor to read its label before you wire. Don't trust a picture's order.
03

The Flyback Diode

Reflection 2

A spinning motor doesn't stop instantly when you cut the power. For a split second, it pushes a burst of electricity backward, like water sloshing back in a pipe after a valve slams shut. That burst can wreck the transistor.

The safe path

A diode placed across the motor's two terminals gives that backward burst a harmless loop to circle through until it dies out. When the motor runs normally, the diode just sits there doing nothing.

Direction matters

A diode is one-way (like the LED in 2.2). Its stripe marks the cathode end. The stripe end points toward the 5V side. Backwards, it would short out the power instead.

05

Controlling Speed with PWM

Parts 2 + 3, Step 4

Pin 9 is a ~ pin. Everything from 2.5 carries over: analogWrite() that dimmed an LED now sets how hard the transistor switch is flicked on and off, and that sets the motor's speed.

const int MOTOR = 9; void setup() { pinMode(MOTOR, OUTPUT); } void loop() { analogWrite(MOTOR, 255); delay(2000); // full speed analogWrite(MOTOR, 128); delay(2000); // about half analogWrite(MOTOR, 0); delay(2000); // stopped }

Try a speed: analogWrite(MOTOR, )

This spins the motor in section 02. Try a very low number like 30 in Tinkercad too (Step 4) and write down what the real simulation does.

The ramp (Part 3, you write it)

  • One for loop: start speed at 0, go up to 255, adding 5 each time with speed += 5. Inside: analogWrite(MOTOR, speed); then delay(50);
  • A second for loop goes back down from 255 to 0, subtracting 5 each time.

+= 5 and -= 5

speed += 5 is a shortcut for speed = speed + 5. speed -= 5 means speed = speed - 5. Counting by 5 from 0 hits 0, 5, 10 … 255 exactly.

Low-number thinking (Reflection 3): a motor has to overcome friction and the weight of its own parts before it can start turning. A very small push might not be enough. Compare what you see in Tinkercad at 30 to what an LED did at 30 in 2.5.

Test yourself

The pin flips the switch. The transistor carries the load. The diode catches the kickback.

Small signal, big power: that's how nearly every Arduino controls anything heavier than an LED.
