10.3 — Break and Continue

🔔 Bell Ringer — Think About It
  1. break immediately exits a loop entirely; continue skips just the current pass and moves to the next one. What's a situation where you'd want to stop a loop completely versus just skip one round of it?
  2. If you were searching through a list looking for one specific item, why might break be useful once you find it?
  3. Why might continue be useful if you're processing a list of numbers but want to skip over any negative ones?
  4. Can you think of a bug that might happen if you use break when you actually meant continue, or the other way around?

Notes

Look up these definitions:

  • break
  • continue

Key idea: break immediately exits a loop entirely, useful once you've found what you're looking for and don't need to keep checking. continue skips just the rest of the current pass through the loop and jumps back to check the condition again, useful when you want to skip specific cases without stopping the whole loop. Mixing these two up is an easy mistake — break stops everything, continue just moves on to the next round — so it's worth being deliberate about which one a situation actually calls for.
