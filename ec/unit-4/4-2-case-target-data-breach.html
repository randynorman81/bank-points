4.2 — Case: Target Data Breach

Intro

Ask AI these questions; look over the answers.

  1. What happened in the 2013 Target data breach? How did attackers actually get in?
  2. Explain to a middle schooler what a point-of-sale (POS) terminal is, and why it's a tempting target for hackers.
  3. Create a simple timeline showing how the attackers went from stealing HVAC vendor credentials to actually stealing credit card numbers from checkout terminals.
  4. Why might a company give a vendor like an HVAC contractor network access in the first place? What's the legitimate reason, and what's the risk?
  5. Connect the idea of an input device (like a checkout terminal) being secretly compromised to a real product or system you might use that also accepts some kind of input.
  6. What did Target end up paying as a result of this breach, and what changes did retailers make afterward?

Notes

Look up these definitions:

  • Point-of-sale (POS) terminal
  • Vendor access
  • Lateral movement
  • Data exfiltration

Background: in 2013, attackers stole network credentials from an HVAC vendor with legitimate remote access to Target's systems, then used lateral movement to reach the point-of-sale terminals themselves, installing malware that captured card data as customers swiped. Roughly 40 million card numbers and 70 million customer records were stolen; Target paid an $18.5 million settlement. A POS terminal is fundamentally an input device — just like the keypad you're about to build. This case is a direct preview of this unit's theme: what happens when the device accepting input is itself compromised?
