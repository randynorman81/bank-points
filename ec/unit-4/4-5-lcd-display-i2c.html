4.5 — LCD Display: I2C Serial Communication

New hardware: the I2C LCD 16x2 display — only 4 wires needed. This is the course's first formal communication protocol.

Intro

Ask AI these questions; look over the answers.

  1. What is I2C, and why can it let multiple devices communicate using only 2 wires, no matter how many devices are connected?
  2. Explain to a middle schooler the difference between a "party line" phone system where everyone shares one line versus everyone having their own separate phone line, and connect that to how I2C works.
  3. Create a simple diagram showing an Arduino connected to multiple I2C devices, all sharing the same 2 wires (SDA and SCL), each with its own unique address.
  4. Why does every device on an I2C bus need a unique address? What would happen if two devices had the same address?
  5. Connect I2C to a real product you might recognize that has multiple small chips or sensors communicating internally.
  6. What does "master" and "slave" mean in the context of I2C communication, and which one is the Arduino usually acting as?

Notes

Look up these definitions and add them to your living glossary:

  • I2C (Inter-Integrated Circuit)
  • SDA (Serial Data)
  • SCL (Serial Clock)
  • I2C address
  • Master/slave

Key idea: I2C lets many devices share just 2 wires — SDA carries the actual data, SCL is a shared clock signal keeping everyone synchronized. Every device has a unique I2C address; the master (the Arduino) includes that address when it wants to talk to a specific device, and only that device responds. Contrast with everything wired so far: the HC-SR04 needed 2 dedicated pins for itself, the keypad needed 8 — I2C devices only ever need 2 signal wires (plus power/ground), no matter how many are on the bus. This matters beyond the LCD: I2C shows up constantly in real products (sensors, real-time clocks, small displays).
