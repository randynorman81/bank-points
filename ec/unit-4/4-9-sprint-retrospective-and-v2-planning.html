4.9 — Sprint Retrospective + V2 Planning

No new building today — bring your Lesson 4.6 code and developer log entries for genuine reflection. This closes out the unit's formal Scrum introduction.

Intro

Ask AI these questions; look over the answers.

  1. What is a sprint retrospective, and why do professional teams hold one even after a successful sprint, not just after a failed one?
  2. Explain to a middle schooler why reflecting honestly on what went wrong is just as valuable as celebrating what went right.
  3. Create a simple template someone could use to structure their own retrospective, covering what went well, what didn't, and what to do differently.
  4. What is a backlog, and how does a team decide what to prioritize from it for a future sprint?
  5. Connect today's idea of reflecting on a finished project to something you do (or could do) after finishing something in your own life — a game, a sport, a project.
  6. Why might honest reflection be harder than it sounds, even when no one is grading you on how positive your reflection is?

Notes

No new vocabulary today. Go back to your Lesson 4.1 entries for Scrum, sprint, standup, and backlog and update their Comfort Rating — now that you've completed a full sprint cycle, your understanding should be more concrete. Don't add new rows.

A retrospective is a structured reflection at the end of a sprint — different from your Developer Log, which captures day-to-day moments. Three questions: (1) What went well? Be specific, not "it went fine." (2) What didn't go well? Be honest — naming friction isn't a confession of failure. (3) What would you do differently? Should connect directly to #2. A superficial retrospective ("everything went well, no problems") is rarely true and never useful. Today you'll also plan — not build — a genuinely new V2 improvement for your PIN system.
