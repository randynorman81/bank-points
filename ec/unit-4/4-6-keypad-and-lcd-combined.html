4.6 — Keypad + LCD Combined

Two-period lesson — the sprint deliverable. Every piece here is review (4.3, 4.4, 4.5); what's new is integration, which is genuinely harder than it sounds.

Intro

Ask AI these questions; look over the answers.

  1. Why might combining two systems that each work perfectly on their own sometimes create new problems that didn't exist before?
  2. Explain to a middle schooler why a team of three people who each do their job well might still struggle if they've never practiced working together.
  3. Create a simple checklist someone could use to debug a problem that only appears when two previously separate systems are combined, not when either runs alone.
  4. What is "integration" in software and hardware engineering, and why is it often considered one of the harder parts of building something?
  5. Connect today's challenge — combining separate working pieces into one system — to a real product you might recognize that clearly combines multiple components working together.
  6. Why might engineers deliberately test each component separately before combining them, rather than building everything together from the very beginning?

Notes

Look up these definitions and add them to your living glossary:

  • Integration
  • Pin conflict
  • User feedback

Key idea: combining working pieces surfaces new problems that never showed up when each ran alone — a real, well-known engineering challenge, not a sign you did something wrong earlier. Watch for: pin conflicts (two components accidentally using the same pin), variable naming collisions between code copied from different lessons, and timing interference from a blocking delay(). Strategy: combine one piece at a time and test after each addition — keypad + LCD first (Day 1), then PIN logic on top (Day 2). User feedback means your LCD can now show readable messages ("Enter PIN," "Access Granted," "Access Denied") instead of just a single LED.
