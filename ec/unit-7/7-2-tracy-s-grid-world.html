7.2 — Tracy's Grid World

🔔 Bell Ringer — Think About It
  1. Tracy lives on a coordinate grid, the same x/y system you use in math class. Why is a grid a useful way to describe exactly where something is on a screen?
  2. The penup() and pendown() commands let Tracy move without drawing a line. Why would a drawing tool need a way to move without leaving a mark?
  3. If Tracy starts at (0, 0), what do you think happens to her position after a few forward() commands?
  4. Coordinate grids show up outside of programming too — maps, spreadsheets, games. Where else have you seen an x/y coordinate system used?

Notes

Look up these definitions:

  • Coordinate plane
  • Coordinate pair (x, y)
  • penup() / pendown()

Key idea: Tracy's world is laid out on a coordinate plane, the same x/y grid from math class, and her position at any moment can be described with a coordinate pair. The penup() and pendown() commands control whether moving Tracy draws a line or not — penup() lifts the "pen" so you can reposition her without adding to the drawing, and pendown() puts it back down. Knowing exactly where Tracy is on the grid at every step is what makes it possible to plan out more complex drawings.
