7.8 — Top Down Design

🔔 Bell Ringer — Think About It
  1. Top down design means breaking a big problem into smaller pieces before writing any code. Why might planning before coding save you time overall, even though it feels slower at first?
  2. If you were asked to draw a full house (walls, roof, door, windows) with Tracy, what smaller pieces would you break that into?
  3. Why might it be easier to test and fix a small function that draws just a door, instead of testing one giant block of code that draws the whole house at once?
  4. This is the same idea as the functions you wrote in Unit 3 and in Codecademy's C++ course. Why do you think this concept keeps coming back across every language and platform you learn?

Notes

Look up these definitions:

  • Top down design
  • Decomposition
  • Function (review)

Key idea: Top down design means starting with the big goal, then breaking it into smaller and smaller sub-problems until each piece is simple enough to solve directly — usually by writing it as its own function. A complicated drawing (like a house) becomes a handful of small functions (draw_wall(), draw_roof(), draw_door()) that are each easy to write and test on their own, then called together to build the whole picture. This is the exact same skill as the functions you wrote in Unit 3 Tinkercad code and in Codecademy's C++ course — breaking a big problem into small, testable pieces is a skill that transfers across every language you'll ever use.
