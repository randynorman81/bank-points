7.4 — Turning Tracy Using Angles

🔔 Bell Ringer — Think About It
  1. Up until now Tracy could only turn in 90-degree steps. Now she can turn by any angle. What new shapes become possible that weren't before?
  2. A hexagon's outer angle is 60 degrees. If you know the math behind a shape's turn angle, what does that let you do that guessing-and-checking doesn't?
  3. Diagonal lines weren't really possible with only 90-degree turns. Why does allowing any angle change that?
  4. If you combined an angle turn with a for loop, what do you think you could draw?

Notes

Look up these definitions:

  • Angle (degrees)
  • Exterior angle
  • Polygon

Key idea: Once Tracy can turn by any angle, not just fixed 90-degree steps, diagonal lines and any regular polygon become possible — a triangle needs 120-degree turns, a hexagon needs 60-degree turns, and the pattern (360 divided by the number of sides) works for any polygon. This is where turning commands and for loops start to work together: a loop that repeats "move forward, turn by an angle" a fixed number of times can draw a whole shape in just two lines of code.
