7.1 — What Is IoT? + AI in IoT Deep Dive

This deepens Unit 1's AI-in-IoT content, not a repeat. No hardware this unit until 7.6.

Intro

Ask AI these questions; look over the answers.

  1. What is the Internet of Things, and how does it differ from just "the internet" in general?
  2. Now that you understand more about embedded systems than you did at the start of this course, ask your AI: how does an individual embedded device, like the ones you've built this year, become part of a larger IoT system?
  3. Create a simple diagram showing several different IoT devices (a thermostat, a camera, a fitness tracker) all connected to the same broader network and cloud infrastructure.
  4. How has AI in IoT devices changed or improved in recent years? Ask your AI specifically about advances since you might have last researched this in an earlier unit.
  5. Connect today's topic to your own RFID access control project from Unit 5 — could that project become a true IoT device, and what would need to change for that to happen?
  6. What are some IoT devices you didn't realize existed when you first learned about this topic earlier this year?

Notes

Look up these definitions:

  • IoT (Internet of Things)
  • Smart device
  • Device ecosystem
  • Data exchange
  • Connectivity

Key idea: IoT means physical, embedded devices connected to a network and each other — not just "anything involving computers." Your traffic light (Unit 2), alarm (Unit 3), PIN entry system (Unit 4), and RFID lock (Unit 5) were all standalone embedded systems — fully functional but not IoT, because none had connectivity. Lesson 7.6's Arduino Cloud lab will be this course's first genuinely connected build. Device ecosystems create convenience (everything works together) but also risk (one vulnerability in the central service can affect every connected device at once) — this sets up 7.5 and 7.7's cases directly.
