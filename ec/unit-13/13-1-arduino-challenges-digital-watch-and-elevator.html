13.1 — Arduino Challenges (Digital Watch & Elevator)

🔔 Bell Ringer — Think About It
  1. The millis() command counts time without pausing your whole program the way delay() does. Why might that matter for something like a digital watch, which needs to keep running while doing other things?
  2. An LCD screen can display text and numbers, unlike a single LED. What new kinds of information could you show a user with a screen that you couldn't before?
  3. A digital watch and an elevator both need to track state (the current time, or the current floor) and update it continuously. What do you think that shares in common with the finite state machine idea in the Bank Points system feel like?
  4. Which of the two projects (digital watch or elevator) sounds more interesting to build, and why?

Notes

Look up these definitions:

  • millis()
  • LCD screen (review from library use)
  • State tracking

Key idea: This lesson introduces millis(), which counts elapsed time without pausing the rest of your program the way delay() does — essential for something like a digital watch that has to keep ticking while still checking buttons or other inputs. Combined with an LCD screen for displaying information, you'll build two classic physical-computing projects: a digital watch and an elevator simulator, both of which depend on tracking and updating a changing state over time.
