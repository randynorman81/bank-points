9.7 — Resume Polish + Job Search Activity

🔔 Bell Ringer — Think About It
  1. The lesson says real specificity, like "Built an RFID-based access control system integrating a servo motor and LCD display," is more credible than exaggeration. Why might a specific, modest-sounding accomplishment actually impress an employer more than a vague, impressive-sounding claim?
  2. You're asked to honestly compare your actual skills against a real job posting's requirements, noting gaps as well as matches. Does admitting what you don't have yet make your application weaker, or does it show something an employer might actually value?
  3. You built this resume back in Unit 1, and today you're leveling it up with a full year of real, demonstrable projects behind it. Looking at both versions side by side, what does the difference actually prove about you?
  4. If two students both list "Arduino programming" as a skill, but one can point to an RFID lock with a working servo and LCD while the other can't describe a single specific project, are they really claiming the same skill? What does that tell you about how employers probably read a resume?

Directions

  1. Add your capstone to your resume with specific, strong action verbs.
  2. Add at least 2 other projects and any missing technical skills.
  3. Find one real, current job posting relevant to this field.
  4. Complete a skill-matching comparison against the posting's requirements.
  5. Submit your updated resume, link/screenshot, comparison, and reflection in Google Classroom.

You're not starting from scratch — you built this resume back in Unit 1, Lesson 1.6. Today you're leveling it up to reflect a full year of real, demonstrable skill.

Notes

Look up these definitions:

  • Skill matching
  • Action verb
  • Entry-level position

Your resume should now reflect every major project (traffic light, alarm, PIN entry, RFID lock, Unit 7 design project, this capstone) and specific technical skills (Arduino programming, sensor integration, debugging methodology, Scrum/sprint planning). Strong language is specific ("Built an RFID-based access control system integrating a servo motor and LCD display," not "Did a project with sensors"), uses action verbs (built, designed, debugged, integrated), and is honest — real specificity is more credible than exaggeration. Skill matching means directly comparing a real job posting's requirements against your actual skills, honestly noting gaps as well as matches.

Assignments

Submit a link/screenshot, a skill-matching comparison (have it / partially have it / don't have it yet for each requirement), and a brief reflection on one skill you'd want to develop further.
