9.4 — Logical Operators

🔔 Bell Ringer — Think About It
  1. Python's logical operators are spelled out as words — and, or, not — instead of symbols like C++'s &&, ||, !. Which style do you find more readable, and why?
  2. Write a rule from your life that needs "and" to join two conditions (both have to be true).
  3. Write a different rule that needs "or" (either one being true is enough).
  4. not flips a boolean's value. If gameOver is False, what does not gameOver evaluate to?

Notes

Look up these definitions:

  • and, or, not (Python logical operators)

Key idea: Python's logical operators do the exact same job as C++'s &&, ||, and ! — combining or flipping boolean conditions — but Python spells them out as the words and, or, and not instead of using symbols. and requires both conditions to be true, or needs just one of them to be true, and not flips a single condition's value. This lets you build compound conditions like if age >= 13 and hasPermissionSlip:, which reads almost like a plain English sentence.
