9.6 — Forms

🔔 Bell Ringer — Think About It
  1. A plain, unstyled <input> looks tiny and cramped by default. Why do you think browsers ship such a plain default style for form fields?
  2. A contact form, a login form, and a search bar all use the exact same handful of Bootstrap form classes. What does that tell you about how "different" those three things really are, underneath?
  3. Every field on a real form usually has a visible <label>, not just placeholder text inside the box. Why does that matter for someone using a screen reader?
  4. Now that you've built a navbar, cards, and a grid, what would a finished form tie together on your own site — a contact page? A sign-up box? Something else?

Notes

Bootstrap styles form fields the same way it styles everything else: add the right class, and the plain browser default becomes a clean, sized input.

<form> <div class="mb-3"> <label class="form-label">Email address</label> <input type="email" class="form-control"> </div> <div class="mb-3"> <label class="form-label">Message</label> <textarea class="form-control"></textarea> </div> <button type="submit" class="btn btn-primary">Send</button> </form>
ClassGoes onWhat it does
form-label<label>Standard label spacing above a field
form-control<input>, <textarea>, <select>Full-width, padded, clean-bordered field
mb-3The wrapping <div> around each fieldMargin-bottom spacing utility (from 9.3-style utility classes), so fields don't crowd each other

The submit button is just a regular Bootstrap button (from 9.3), with type="submit".

Look up these terms if anything above is unclear: form-control, form-label, submit button.
