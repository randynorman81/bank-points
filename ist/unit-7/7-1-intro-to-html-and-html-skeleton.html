Exit Ticket

Three quick questions. Click the answer you think is right.

1. Where does the <title> tag go?

Inside the <head>. The title is information about the page (it shows in the browser tab), not part of the page you see. Putting it in the body is the most common first mistake.

2. You type <h1>My Site but forget the </h1>. What is most likely to happen?

The browser keeps going. Without the closing tag it has no stop sign, so the heading can spill onto everything that follows. That is why every opening tag needs its closing tag.

3. In your code, you press Enter in the middle of a paragraph. What shows on the page?

HTML ignores line breaks and extra spaces. The browser wraps text to fit the width of the window. It does not care where you pressed Enter.

7.1.1 Your First Website: A Wikipedia-Style Page

Build a plain, text-heavy web page about a topic you know or want to learn about, like a mini encyclopedia article. Pick anything school-appropriate: an animal, a sport, a game, a place, a person, a food, a band.

Your page must have:

  • The skeleton, starting with <!DOCTYPE html> on line 1, then html, head, and body
  • A <title> inside the head (it shows in the browser tab)
  • Exactly one <h1>: the name of your topic
  • At least 10 paragraphs (<p>). At least 8 of them should be 25 words or longer
  • At least 300 words in all
  • Every tag closed and nested correctly
  • Only these tags: html, head, title, body, h1, and p

Yes, it will look plain and text-heavy. That is on purpose! In the next lessons you will make this same page look great with headings, colors, and more.

Plan your paragraphs. An encyclopedia article usually covers things in an order like this:

  1. Introduction: what it is, in a few sentences
  2. Background or history
  3. What it looks like or how it works
  4. More details (two or three paragraphs)
  5. Why it matters
  6. Challenges or problems
  7. A fun fact
  8. Sources: a last paragraph listing at least two places you learned from

Writing rules: write everything in your own words. Do not copy and paste from Wikipedia or any other site. Use at least two sources and list them in your last paragraph.
