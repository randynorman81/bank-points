Borders

A border is a picture frame for your content. Learn the six styles, then learn to frame just one side to make underlines, dividers, and accents.

border · border styles · one-sided borders
01

Border Formula Recap

You met border in 7.2. It is a style property, so it always lives inside the shell: style=" ". It has three parts in order: thickness, style, color.

style="border: 4px solid navy;"
No style word, no border.border: 4px navy; draws nothing. The style (solid, dashed...) is the part the browser needs most.
02

The Six Border Styles

The middle part of the shortcut picks the look. Click a style to see all six live at your chosen thickness.

StyleLooks likeGood for
solidOne unbroken lineClean, everyday frames
dashedA row of dashesCoupons, cut here lines
dottedA row of dotsSoft, playful dividers
doubleTwo parallel linesCertificates, old-time posters
grooveLooks carved into the page3D "engraved" boxes
ridgeLooks raised off the page3D "embossed" boxes

double, groove, and ridge need at least 3px to look right. At 1px they just look like a plain line.

03

One Side at a Time

Sometimes you do not want a full frame. A newspaper headline gets a line underneath. A quote gets a bar on the left. To frame just one side, use border-top, border-right, border-bottom, or border-left. They use the same three parts.

Toggle the sides and see the code.

04

Put It Together

Borders work best with the properties you already know.

Code

<h1 style="color: maroon; border-bottom: 4px double maroon;">WANTED</h1> <p style="background-color: tan; border: 6px ridge saddlebrown;"> Reward: $500 </p>
