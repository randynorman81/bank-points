Editing Tags, Span & Font Families

Four tags that change how words look, one tag that does nothing until you tell it to, and the three font families every font belongs to. Together they let you style part of a sentence, not just the whole thing.

Bold · Italic · hr · br · span · font-family
01

Editing Tags

Editing tags change how text looks or how a page is laid out, without any style attribute. Click each one.

Bold

Wrap text in <b> or <strong>. It has an opening tag and a closing tag.

<p>This is <b>important</b> text.</p>

Shows: This is important text.

<b> just makes it look bold. <strong> means it is important, which also helps screen readers. Both look the same.

Italic

Wrap text in <i> or <em>. Opening tag and closing tag again.

<p>I <i>love</i> this song.</p>

Shows: I love this song.

<em> means emphasis, like stressing a word when you say it. Use it for titles, thoughts, or words you want to lean on.

Horizontal line

<hr> draws a line across the page to separate sections. It has only one tag. There is nothing to wrap, so there is no closing tag.

<h2>Appetizers</h2> <hr> <p>Fries</p>

Line break

<br> ends the line and starts the next one, like pressing Enter. It also has only one tag.

<p>Roses are red,<br>Violets are blue.</p>

Shows:
Roses are red,
Violets are blue.

Pressing Enter in your code does not break the line on the page. Only <br> does.

02

The Span Tag

A highlighter pen does nothing until you drag it across a word. The <span> tag works the same way. On its own it changes nothing at all. Add a style attribute and it styles only the words you wrapped.

Code

<p>My favorite color is <span style="color: tomato;">tomato</span>. </p>

What the browser shows

My favorite color is tomato.

Fact 01

It styles part of a line

Put the style on a <p> and the whole paragraph changes. Put it on a <span> and only the wrapped words change.

Fact 02

It stays in the line

A span does not start a new line. It sits right inside the sentence, which is why it is called an inline tag.

Fact 03

It has no look of its own

Without a style attribute, a span is invisible. It is a blank highlighter waiting for you.

Fact 04

It goes inside another tag

A span lives inside a <p>, <h1>, or another block. Open it, wrap the words, close it.

Be the highlighter

Pick a color, then click words in the sentence. Each word you click gets its own span. Click a word again to remove it.

03

The 3 Basic Font Families

There are thousands of fonts, but they all fall into just three families. If a computer does not have the font you asked for, it falls back to the family and picks something similar. That is why the family is the safe choice.

Common mistakes

Wrong: closing a one-tag tag

Line one</br>Line two

Right

Line one<br>Line two

Wrong: forgot to close bold

<b>Sale</p>

Right

<b>Sale</b>

Wrong: font-family with no shell

<p font-family: serif;>

Right

<p style="font-family: serif;">

Wrong: span with no style

<span>red</span>

Right: give it a style

<span style="color: red;">red</span>
