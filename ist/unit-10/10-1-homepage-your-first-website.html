6.1 — Homepage: Your First Website

3 weeks: plan + build (week 1), keep building (week 2), finish, submit, and showcase (week 3).

🔔 Bell Ringer — Think About It
  1. Every real website you visit was built by combining dozens of small skills — a heading here, a hover effect there, a navbar, a form. Which single skill from this whole year do you think will show up the most in your own site?
  2. A real website almost never has just one page. Why do you think even a simple personal site is expected to have more than one?
  3. You're about to pick your own topic for this site. What's one thing that makes a website feel like "yours" instead of a generic template?
  4. You'll submit this in CodeHS and show it off in a class showcase. Why might building something knowing other people will see it change how carefully you build it?

Notes

This is a project day — no new vocab. This is your capstone: one real, multi-page website that proves you can use everything from the HTML unit, the CSS unit, and the Bootstrap unit together, on your own, with no step-by-step instructions telling you which tag goes where.

Pick Your Site (choose one, or pitch your own to Mr. Norman)

  • A personal portfolio — about you, your interests, and things you've made or accomplished
  • A fan site — for a game, show, movie, band, or artist you're into
  • A small business concept — a made-up shop, restaurant, or service, like a real company's site
  • A hobby or team site — a sport, club, or hobby you're part of
  • Something weird and fully your own — as long as it meets every requirement below, the topic is wide open

Whatever you pick, every requirement below has to show up somewhere on your site — the topic just decides what the content actually says.

Requirements (the minimums — you can always add more)

Structure — at least 3 pages

  • A homepage that introduces the site and links to your other pages
  • 2 or more additional pages, each linked from the homepage (and linking back)
  • Every page has a real title tag, a heading 1, and a consistent look across all pages (same nav, same color scheme)

HTML unit skills — somewhere on the site

  • Headings, paragraphs, bold and italic text, at least one list (bullet or numbered)
  • At least 2 images, each with a real alt description and a set width
  • At least one table used for real tabular data (not for page layout)
  • The box model in action: padding, border, and margin used on purpose, not by accident

CSS unit skills — in your own style.css

  • All of your styling lives in an external style.css file, linked from every page — no style attributes doing the heavy lifting
  • At least one rule using a class and one using an id
  • At least one hover effect with a smooth transition (a link, a button, or a card)
  • At least one section laid out with flexbox or grid

Bootstrap unit skills — somewhere on the site

  • The Bootstrap link in every page's head
  • A navbar that's the same across every page, with real working links between your pages
  • Bootstrap's grid system (container, row, columns) used for at least one section
  • At least 2 cards
  • A form with at least 2 labeled fields and a submit button (a contact form, a sign-up form, a search box — your choice, it doesn't need to actually submit anywhere real)
These are the minimums. A site that only hits the minimums will look thin — the best sites this year went well past this list. Use color, spacing, and real content to make it look like a site you'd actually want to visit.

Directions

  1. Week 1 — Plan + build: pick your topic, sketch what your 3+ pages will be, then start building your homepage. Get your navbar and style.css linked on every page from day one — it's much harder to add that later.
  2. Week 2 — Keep building: finish your remaining pages. Work through the requirements list above like a checklist — don't leave the form, the hover effect, or the flexbox/grid section for the last minute.
  3. Week 3 — Finish, submit, showcase: fix anything broken, double-check every requirement is actually on the site (not just planned), submit in CodeHS, and be ready to show your site to the class.

Assignments

Submitted and graded through your CodeHS sandbox, using the requirements above.

  • 9.1.3 Setting up your CodeHS domain (200 points, formative)
  • 9.1.4 Create your homepage (200 points, summative)
