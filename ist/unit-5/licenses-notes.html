Who Owns Software? Licenses, Piracy & Open Source

You never actually buy most software. You buy permission. The fine print decides what you can do, and breaking it has real consequences.

Georgia IT-IST-11.4 · IT-IST-11.5 · IT-IST-11.6
00

You Bought a Permission Slip

When you click “I Agree” on a wall of text, you've signed a contract. Software is protected by copyright the moment it's written, and the owner decides the rules for using it. A software license is that rulebook: it says who can use the software, on how many devices, for how long, and whether you can change or share it.

Buying a book vs. buying software: you own the physical book and can lend or resell it. With software you own a license to use it — the company still owns the software itself.
01

The License Spectrum

Licenses run from “locked down” to “do whatever you want.” Click each type.

Proprietary (commercial)

You pay for the right to use it, the source code is secret, and you can't copy or modify it. Examples: Microsoft Office, Adobe Photoshop, most console games.

Shareware / Freemium

Free to try, then pay to keep using it or to unlock the good stuff. Examples: a 30-day trial, or a free game with paid upgrades.

Freeware

Costs nothing, but the code is still owned and closed. You can use it but not change or resell it. Examples: Zoom's free tier, many small utilities.

Open source

The source code is public. You can read it, change it, and share it, under conditions set by the license (MIT and GPL are two famous ones). Examples: Linux, Firefox, Python, VS Code's core.

Free as in freedom — not always free as in price.

Public domain

No owner, no rules. Anyone can do anything with it. The rarest category for modern software.

02

Piracy Has Consequences

Software piracy is using, copying, or distributing software without permission. It's a form of copyright infringement, and the risks are bigger than a fine.

Risk 01

Legal

Civil damages and, at scale, criminal charges. Businesses get audited.

Risk 02

Malware

Cracked installers are a favorite hiding place for trojans and ransomware. The “free” copy can cost you everything.

Risk 03

No updates

Pirated copies can't safely receive patches (Lesson 5.6).

Risk 04

Real people

Indie developers and small studios live on those sales.

Not piracy: free trials, student licenses, open-source downloads, and legitimate free tiers. Plagiarism is a cousin problem — passing off someone else's code or work as your own. Both are “inappropriate use” with consequences.
