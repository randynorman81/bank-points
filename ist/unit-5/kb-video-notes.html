How to Make a KB Article Video

A KB article can be words on a page, or it can be a video that shows every click. You will script it, storyboard it, record it, and star in it. This page walks you through all of it.

Project IT Professional
00

What Is a KB Article Video?

Some problems are easier to see than to read. If someone asks how to share a Google Doc, you could write ten steps, or you could record your screen and show them. A KB article video is a knowledge base article that moves: a short screen-recorded walkthrough of how to fix or do something, with your voice explaining each step.

Term 01

Script

The exact words you will say in the video, written out ahead of time so you do not ramble or forget a step.

Term 02

Storyboard

A row of sketches showing what the viewer sees at each key moment, like a comic strip of your video.

Term 03

Video production

The whole process of making a video: planning, writing, sketching, recording, checking, and sharing.

The script is what you say. The storyboard is what they see. The video is both together.A KB article video still has the same six parts as a written article: Title, Symptom, Cause, Steps, Check It Worked, and Still Stuck.
01

The Six-Step Pipeline

Professionals do not just hit record. Click through the steps in order.

🎯

Plan

Pick one problem and one audience. Make it specific: "how to connect a Chromebook to school Wi-Fi for a new 7th grader" beats "how to use the internet."

You must be able to do the process yourself, from start to finish, without looking anything up.

📝

Script

Write what you will say, section by section: intro, symptom, cause, each step, check it worked, still stuck, outro. Use short sentences and plain words.

Read it out loud. If you stumble, rewrite that sentence.

🖼️

Storyboard

Sketch one frame for each key moment: what is on the screen, and what you say. You will find gaps in your plan here, which is much cheaper than finding them after recording.

🎙️

Record

Clean up your screen, test your microphone, do one practice run, then record. Speak slowly, and say what you click as you click it.

🧪

Test

Have a classmate follow only your video. Wherever they pause, replay, or ask a question, your video has a hole. Fix it, or re-record that part.

📤

Share

Put your script, storyboard, and video in one Google folder. Set it to public (anyone with the link can view), then share the folder link with Mr. Norman.

02

What a Script Looks Like

A good script has two columns: the words you say and what is on the screen while you say them. Here is a small sample for a video called "Fix 'No Internet' on a Chromebook."

SectionWhat I sayWhat is on the screen
Intro + Title"Hi, I'm Sam. In this video, I'll show you how to fix a Chromebook that says No Internet."Title slide with the video name
Symptom"You open a web page, and it says No Internet, even though other people are online."The "No Internet" error screen
Cause"This usually means your Chromebook lost its Wi-Fi connection."Wi-Fi icon showing no bars
Step 1"First, click the clock in the bottom right corner of your screen."Cursor clicks the clock
Step 2"Next, click the Wi-Fi icon, and make sure the button is blue, which means it is on."The Wi-Fi panel, zoomed in
Check it worked"Now open a new tab. If a website loads, you're fixed."A website loading
Still stuck"If it still says No Internet, take your Chromebook to the help desk and tell them what you saw."Text on screen: "Help desk"

One frame per key moment. Stick figures and boxes are fine. What matters is that the plan is clear.

If you can't draw it, you can't film it.A storyboard problem is a five-minute fix. A recording problem can mean starting over.
04

Recording Tips

Tip 01

Clean your screen first

Close extra tabs, hide personal bookmarks, and turn on Do Not Disturb so no messages pop up mid-recording.

Tip 02

Never show private info

No passwords, private messages, or personal details on screen. Use a practice account if you need to log in.

Tip 03

Slow down

You already know where to click, but the viewer does not. Move the mouse slowly and pause after each step.

Tip 04

Say what you click

Say "Click the Wi-Fi icon" as your cursor gets there. The viewer hears and sees the same thing.

Tip 05

Test your audio

Record ten seconds and listen back before the real take. A great video with bad audio is a bad video.

Tip 06

Mistakes happen

If you stumble, pause, then say the sentence again. Or start the step over and trim the mistake out. Check your tool's time limit before you begin.

05

Good vs. Bad KB Videos

Bad KB video

  • Starts recording with no intro or title.
  • Says "um" and rambles, with no script.
  • Clicks fast, and the viewer cannot follow.
  • Says "just go to settings" without showing where.
  • Uses jargon without explaining it.
  • Just stops. No check, no next step.

Good KB video

  • Opens with a clear title and the problem.
  • Follows a script and speaks calmly.
  • Moves slowly and pauses after each step.
  • Shows and says exactly what to click.
  • Explains new words in plain language.
  • Ends with "Check it worked" and "Still stuck?"

06

Before You Submit

  • My video is 2 to 4 minutes long.
  • All six KB parts appear: Title, Symptom, Cause, Steps, Check It Worked, Still Stuck.
  • Each step is one action, and I say and show exactly what I click.
  • My voice is clear and my audio is easy to hear.
  • Nothing private shows on my screen.
  • A classmate followed my video without asking me questions.
  • My script, storyboard, and video are in one public Google folder, and I shared the link with Mr. Norman.
