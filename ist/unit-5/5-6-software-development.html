5.6 — Software Development

🔔 Bell Ringer — Think About It
  1. Waterfall plans everything out before writing a single line of code; Agile builds, tests, and changes direction constantly. Which approach would actually scare you more to work under as a new employee, and why?
  2. If you used Waterfall to build a new Spotify feature and users hated it after six months of planning, whose fault is that — the method, or the team that chose it?
  3. Agile means constantly changing your plan based on feedback. At what point does "being flexible" turn into "never actually finishing anything"?
  4. A brand-new employee trying to "move up in the company," like the notes describe — would picking Agile or Waterfall make them look more impressive to their boss? Does the "right" method depend on the project, or on how you want to be seen?

Directions

  1. Look up today's vocabulary: SDLC, Waterfall, and Agile.
  2. Compare how the Waterfall and Agile methods would each work for building a new feature at a company like Spotify.
  3. Complete today's assignment.
  4. Submit it in Google Classroom.

Notes

Look up these definitions:

  • SDLC (Software Development Life Cycle)
  • Waterfall model
  • Agile model

Imagine you're a new employee at Spotify, required to build a new feature using either the Waterfall or Agile method. Compare how both methods would work, then decide which one makes more sense and would help you move up in the company.
