What Is a KB Article?

Every time you Google "how do I fix..." and land on a clear, step-by-step page, you are reading one. Behind that page is an IT writer whose whole job was to make a confusing problem simple.

Project IT Professional
00

Write It Down Once

Imagine you work at a help desk. Someone asks how to fix a "Page Not Found" error. You explain it. Twenty minutes later, someone else asks. Then another. By Friday you have explained it forty times. A smart IT team fixes this by writing the answer down once, clearly, and sharing it with everyone.

Term 01

Knowledge base (KB)

An organized library of answers to common problems. Users and support staff search it to solve problems fast, without calling anyone.

Term 02

KB article

One page in the knowledge base that solves one specific problem, step by step. One article, one problem.

Term 03

IT support documentation

The big umbrella term for all written help: KB articles, user guides, FAQs, setup instructions, and troubleshooting checklists.

A KB article is a recipe for fixing a problem.A good recipe works for someone who has never cooked. A good KB article works for someone who knows nothing about computers.
01

The Six Parts of a KB Article

Nearly every good KB article follows the same pattern. Click through the six parts.

🏷️

Title

Says exactly what the article fixes, in the words a user would actually search. Start with an action word.

Good: "How to Fix a 404 Page Not Found Error"
Weak: "Web Issues"

👀

Symptom

What the user actually sees or experiences. This is how a reader decides, "yes, this is my problem."

Example: "You click a link and a page that says 404 or Page Not Found appears instead of the website."

🔍

Cause

A short, plain-English explanation of why it happens. One or two sentences. Understanding the cause helps people trust the fix.

Example: "The website is working, but the page you asked for is not there. The address may have a typo, or the page may have been moved or deleted."

🪜

Steps

The heart of the article. A numbered list where each step is one action, in order, using the exact name of what to click or type.

Example: "1. Look at the web address at the top of the browser. 2. Check it for typing mistakes."

✅

Check It Worked

Tells the reader what they should see if the fix worked, so they are not left guessing.

Example: "The page loads and the 404 message is gone."

🆘

Still Stuck

What to do if the fix fails: who to contact and what information to give them. No reader should hit a dead end.

Example: "Email the website owner and include the full web address that gave you the error."

02

Good vs. Bad

Both articles below "fix" the same problem: a Chromebook that says "No Internet." Only one would actually help a beginner.

Bad KB article

Internet problems are usually caused by network issues. Try resetting your network settings or reconfiguring your DNS and it should work. If not, maybe the router is having problems. Restart it. Then reboot and try again.

Good KB article

Fix "No Internet" on a Chromebook

Symptom: A web page will not open and the screen says "No Internet."

Steps:

  1. Click the clock in the bottom right corner.
  2. Click the Wi-Fi icon and make sure it is turned on.
  3. Click your school's Wi-Fi network name.
  4. Type the password if asked, then click Connect.

Check it worked: A new website loads.

Why is the first one bad? Tap each card.

03

Rules for Great KB Writing

Rule 01

One problem per article

If an article fixes two problems, split it into two articles. Short and focused beats long and crowded.

Rule 02

Write for a beginner

Assume the reader knows nothing. If you must use a technical word, explain it in the same sentence.

Rule 03

One action per step

"Open Settings and click Wi-Fi" is two steps. Split it.

Rule 04

Name exactly what to click

Say "Click the Wi-Fi icon in the bottom right," not "go to your network stuff."

Rule 05

Say what the reader should see

Confirm each big moment: "A list of networks appears." Readers relax when the screen matches your words.

Rule 06

Test it on a real person

If a friend cannot follow it without asking you questions, it is not finished. Fix what confused them.

You will fail on the first draft. That is normal.If you are still confused after your first draft, you do not understand the problem well enough yet. Keep breaking it down until you could explain it to a beginner. Writing the article is how you learn it.
04

A Finished Example

Here is a complete KB article, with all six parts. Use it as a model for your own.

How to Fix "Printer Offline" on a School Chromebook

Symptom

You click Print, but the printer's name shows "Offline" and nothing comes out.

Cause

The Chromebook and the printer are not connected to the same Wi-Fi network right now. This can happen after the printer is turned off or moved.

Steps
  1. Make sure the printer is turned on and the light is solid, not blinking.
  2. Click the Wi-Fi icon in the bottom right corner of your screen.
  3. Check the network name. It should match the name on the printer's screen.
  4. Click Print again and choose the printer from the list.
Check it worked

The printer's name no longer says "Offline," and your page starts printing.

Still stuck?

Ask your teacher to submit a help ticket. Include the printer's name and room number.
