How Software Gets Built: Waterfall vs. Agile

Nobody opens a laptop and types Instagram into existence. Big software gets built by following a process — and the process you pick decides whether the project succeeds.

Georgia IT-IST-7.1 · IT-IST-7.2
00

The Six Steps Every Project Follows

Picture building a house. You don't start hammering. First you decide what the family needs, then you draw blueprints, then you build, then you inspect, then you hand over the keys, then you fix the leaky faucet next spring. Software is no different. This is the software development life cycle (SDLC).

01

Two Ways to Run the Steps

Waterfall

Finish each step completely, then move to the next, like water falling down steps. No going back.

Good for: projects where requirements are fixed and mistakes are very expensive (bridges, medical devices, spacecraft software).

Weakness: you may not see a working product until the very end, and changes are painful.

Agile (Scrum)

Build in short cycles called sprints (1–4 weeks). Each sprint ends with a working piece of the product, then the team reviews it with the customer and adjusts.

Scrum vocab: product backlog (the to-do list), sprint, daily stand-up (a 15-minute check-in), sprint review.

Good for: apps and games where users' needs keep changing.

Weakness: needs constant communication, and scope can creep.

Iterative & incremental

Incremental: build the product in pieces, adding one working chunk at a time. Iterative: build a rough version, then repeatedly improve it. Agile combines both ideas.
