1.4 — Copyright and Ethics

Intro

Ask AI to explain what copyright is and to give you 6 examples of copyright rules being broken by teenagers — 3 that are easy to understand, and 3 that you probably don't know about.

Notes & Examples

📂 The Copyright Case File (today's notes)
Read through all six cases — what's protected, the fair-use test, public domain & Creative Commons, where teens actually break the rules, plagiarism vs. copyright, and what happens if you get it wrong. Today's assignment below pulls straight from it.

Today's Assignment: Copyright or Not? Case Files

One 45-minute class period. Access this in Google Classroom. Read The Copyright Case File first, then work the 8 cases below.

TimeStepWhat You'll Do
0–10 minReadWork through The Copyright Case File if you haven't already.
10–35 minInvestigateWork the 8 cases below on your own or with a partner.
35–45 minRevealClass discussion — verdicts get revealed and disagreements get argued out.

For each case, write a verdict — Clear Infringement, Fair Use, Public Domain / CC, Plagiarism (not copyright), or Grey Area / Tolerated — and one sentence explaining why, using a term from today's notes (a fair-use factor, public domain, a CC license, etc.).

Case 1
You use a NASA photo of Jupiter in a slideshow about space and post it on your public blog.
Case 2
You record yourself reacting to a 15-minute video essay, playing almost the entire thing with only a few comments.
Case 3
You write new, original comedy lyrics making fun of school stereotypes over the exact instrumental beat of a popular song, and post it publicly.
Case 4
You copy two full paragraphs from a news article word-for-word into your essay, then add a footnote citing the site.
Case 5
You use a "CC BY-NC" (Creative Commons, non-commercial) photo in a flyer for a school club's charity bake sale, and credit the photographer.
Case 6
Your friend draws original fan art of a video game character and posts it, tagging the game's official account.
Case 7
You use a clip from a film first released in 1922 in a video project, with no permission and no credit.
Case 8
You write your own short story using the same general "chosen one saves the world" plot idea as a famous book series — different characters, different setting, your own words.
