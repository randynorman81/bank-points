📋 Rules of Engagement (today's notes)
Covers white hat vs. grey hat vs. black hat, what a real penetration test scope agreement looks like, Hack the Pentagon & bug bounties, and why the law (the CFAA) punishes unauthorized access even when nothing gets damaged. Today's assignment below pulls straight from it.

Today's Assignment: Classify the Operation

One 45-minute class period. Access this in Google Classroom. Read Rules of Engagement first, then work the 8 cases below.

TimeStepWhat You'll Do
0–10 minReadWork through Rules of Engagement if you haven't already.
10–35 minClassifyWork the 8 cases below on your own or with a partner.
35–45 minRevealClass discussion — classifications get revealed and disagreements get argued out.

For each case, write a classification — Green (authorized), Amber (grey area), or Red (unauthorized) — and one sentence explaining why, using a term from today's notes (scope agreement, CFAA, authorization, responsible disclosure, etc.).

Case 1
You find a bug bounty program for a video game you play and report a real glitch, following the program's posted rules exactly.
Case 2
You use a friend's account without asking, "just to see if you could guess the password," and don't do anything after logging in.
Case 3
Your school's IT department announces a Capture the Flag night and invites students to try to break into a practice server they set up for it.
Case 4
You find a security flaw in an app with no bug bounty program, and instead of reporting it, you post the exploit publicly on social media.
Case 5
During an authorized penetration test, you find login credentials in an unrelated file you weren't scoped to look at, and you read through it out of curiosity.
Case 6
You take an online ethical-hacking course and practice on a legal, intentionally-vulnerable training site built for that exact purpose.
Case 7
Someone hacks a company to "expose" behavior they see as unethical, then leaks the company's internal documents to the news.
Case 8
You find a vulnerability on your school's public website (not the internal network) and privately email the technology department to report it, without exploiting it further.
