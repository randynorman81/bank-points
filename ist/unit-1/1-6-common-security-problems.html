1.6 — Common Security Problems

🔔 Bell Ringer — Think About It
  1. A scammer's fake bank website can have a totally valid HTTPS padlock, since HTTPS only proves nobody can eavesdrop on the connection — it says nothing about whether the site itself is honest. If the one symbol most people trust to mean "safe" doesn't actually mean that, what should replace it in people's heads?
  2. SMS text codes are the weakest form of 2FA because of SIM-swapping, yet they're still the most common one banks and schools use. If security experts already know a stronger option exists, why do you think most companies still default to the weakest one?
  3. OWASP's #1 most common website flaw is "broken access control" — often just someone changing one number in a web address to see another student's information. Does the fact that it's this easy make it more or less okay to try, out of curiosity, on a site you use every day?
  4. CAPTCHA exists to slow down bots, not humans — yet real humans are the ones who lose a few seconds solving it, every single time, on every site. Is that a fair trade-off, or is there a better way to tell bots and humans apart that just hasn't been built yet?
