This is exactly how real phishing works. You didn't get "hacked" — nothing broke, no password was guessed or cracked. You just typed your real credentials directly into a page built to look like the real thing, and it worked because the page looked convincing enough. The only reason this was safe is that this whole page told you up front it was fake. A real phishing page never does that — it copies a real company's logo, colors, and layout exactly, and hopes you don't look closely enough to notice you're not actually on their site.

What Should Have Tipped You Off? (On a Real Phishing Site)

This page told you outright it was fake. A real attacker's page won't. Here's what to actually check:

The actual URL

A real bank's login lives on their exact domain. A phishing link often uses a lookalike domain, an unrelated domain, or a shortened link hiding where it really goes.

How you got the link

Did you type the bank's address yourself, or click a link from a text/email you weren't expecting? Legitimate logins rarely start from an unsolicited link.

Urgency and pressure

"Your account will be suspended in 1 hour" is a classic phishing pressure tactic designed to make you skip checking the URL.

What happens after you "sign in"

A real login either works or gives a real error. A phishing page often just redirects you to the real site afterward, so you never notice anything was wrong.
