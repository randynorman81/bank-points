1.6 — How Hackers Find Your Identity and Location Online (Including Phones, Again)

🔔 Bell Ringer — Think About It
  1. Every phone sensor exists for a genuinely good reason — the barometer helps 911 find you in a tall building, the accelerometer detects a crash. But those same sensors can guess your income or identify you by how you walk. Should a company be allowed to use a sensor for anything beyond its original safety purpose, just because it's already built into your phone?
  2. When you open an app, your location gets broadcast to about a thousand companies in under a blink of an eye, and the 999 that don't win the ad still keep your data. Does it change how you feel about targeted ads, knowing that "losing" the auction doesn't mean a company forgets what it learned about you?
  3. Normally the government needs a warrant to track your location. But because data brokers sell that same location data on the open market, agencies can just buy it instead — no warrant required. Is that actually different from the government tracking you directly, or the exact same thing with extra steps?
  4. Incognito mode was never built to hide you from websites — only from other people using your own device. If most people think Incognito makes them anonymous online, and it doesn't, whose job is it to correct that?
