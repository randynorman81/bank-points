1.3 — Police and the 4th Amendment

🔔 Bell Ringer — Think About It
  1. In Georgia, police are legally allowed to lie to you during an interrogation — like falsely claiming they already have your fingerprints or a confession from a friend. Does letting police deceive suspects make the justice system better at catching guilty people, or more likely to trap innocent ones?
  2. The 4th Amendment protects you from "unreasonable" searches and seizures — but it never actually defines what counts as unreasonable. Who should get to decide where that line is: police in the moment, a judge after the fact, or voters through the laws they pass?
  3. If police deception is legal partly because a truly innocent person "shouldn't" confess to something they didn't do, what does it say about the law that false confessions under pressure actually happen fairly often?
  4. Imagine you're voting on a law that expands what police are allowed to say during questioning. Would you vote based on protecting the innocent, catching the guilty, or something else — and can one law actually do both at the same time?
