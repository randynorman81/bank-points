1.7 — Copyright and Ethics

🔔 Bell Ringer — Think About It
  1. A platform-licensed song only works inside that app — the second you download the video and repost it elsewhere, that license disappears even though the video looks identical. Does that seem like a fair rule, or a technicality designed to catch people who don't know it exists?
  2. Fair use lets you use a clip if it's "transformative" — but the line between a genuine parody and just reposting something with a joke slapped on is blurry. Who should get to make that call: the platform's algorithm, the original creator, or a court?
  3. The U.S. Copyright Office says a fully AI-generated image can't be copyrighted by anyone — meaning it was never really "yours" even if you typed the prompt. If AI art can't legally belong to anyone, should artists whose work trained the AI still get paid, or does "nobody owns it" cut both ways?
  4. Copying a paragraph and citing it stops plagiarism at school but does nothing to stop a copyright claim. Why do you think the legal system and your school judge the exact same act so differently?
