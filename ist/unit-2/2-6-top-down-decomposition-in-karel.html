2.6 — Top Down Design and Decomposition in Karel

🔔 Bell Ringer — Think About It
  1. Top-down design means planning the big picture first and filling in the small details later, instead of the other way around. Why might starting with tiny details before you know the big plan actually cause more problems than it solves?
  2. If you had to build "The Two Towers" without any top-down decomposition — just writing one giant list of moves with no smaller functions at all — what do you think would go wrong first: writing it, reading it back, or fixing a mistake in it?
  3. A general contractor building a house plans "foundation, frame, walls, roof" before worrying about paint colors. How is that the same idea as top-down decomposition in code, and where do you think that comparison actually breaks down?
  4. Why do you think the main function always goes at the very top of the code, even though it's usually the last piece a programmer actually finishes writing?
