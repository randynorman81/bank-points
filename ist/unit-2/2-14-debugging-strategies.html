2.14 — Debugging Strategies

🔔 Bell Ringer — Think About It
  1. You're handed four already-broken programs and told to fix them, not write them from scratch. Is reading someone else's broken code and figuring out what they were trying to do harder or easier than just writing your own program the right way the first time?
  2. If there are four different major debugging strategies, why do you think no single one of them works best for every kind of bug? What might make a programmer choose one approach over another?
  3. "Random Hurdles" means the hurdles aren't always in the same place. If your debugged program only works for the specific hurdle positions you tested, but breaks on a new random setup, was it actually fixed at all?
  4. Professional programmers debugging massive, real-world software can't just re-read every line — the code is too long. What do you think a strategy that works on a 10-line Karel program would need to change to work on a program with 10,000 lines?
