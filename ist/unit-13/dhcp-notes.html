DHCP

Nobody types in a private IP address by hand when their phone joins Wi-Fi. DHCP is the router quietly handing one out automatically — on a timer.

DHCP · lease · automatic addressing
01

Automatic, and Temporary

Imagine a hotel that gave every guest their room key forever, even after checkout. Within a month, there'd be no rooms left for new guests. Instead, hotels hand out room access for exactly the length of the stay, then take it back so it can be reused. Your router does the same thing with private IP addresses — it's called DHCP (Dynamic Host Configuration Protocol), and each address is handed out as a temporary lease, not a permanent assignment.

02

Why Leases Matter

Reason 1

No manual setup

Nobody has to type in a private IP address for every new device — it just works the moment you connect.

Reason 2

No address runs out

When a device leaves or its lease expires, that address goes back into the pool to be reused by the next device.

Reason 3

Addresses can change

If your phone reconnects after being away, it might get handed a different address than last time — completely normal.

A coffee shop's Wi-Fi can serve hundreds of different laptops in one day without running out of addresses.Every laptop that leaves eventually has its lease expire, freeing that same address for the next customer's laptop.
