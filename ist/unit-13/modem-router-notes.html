Modems and Routers

Most homes today have one box that handles Wi-Fi, but it's secretly doing two completely different jobs. Split them apart to see what each one actually does.

modem · router · gateway · LAN
01

Two Jobs, One Box

A modem is a translator. The signal coming in from your ISP — over fiber, cable, or a phone line — isn't in a format your laptop understands. The modem converts it into data your home network can use, and back again. A router is a traffic director. It takes that one converted connection and splits it, wirelessly and over ethernet, to every device in your house, each getting its own private address.

02

Why They're Usually One Box Today

Most ISPs now rent or sell a single "gateway" device that combines both jobs for convenience. But they're still two separate functions underneath — some people buy their own separate modem and router instead, often to get better Wi-Fi coverage or avoid a monthly rental fee.

Job 1

Modem: translator

Converts the ISP's incoming signal into data your home network understands, and back again. Only one modem is needed — it doesn't create a network by itself.

Job 2

Router: traffic director

Creates your actual home network (the LAN), hands out addresses to each device, and directs traffic between your devices and the modem.

If your modem fails, the internet itself is gone. If your router fails, your devices can't even reach each other.That's the cleanest way to tell which one actually broke when Wi-Fi stops working.
03

Vocab You Need
