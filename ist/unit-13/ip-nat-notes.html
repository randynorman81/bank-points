Public vs. Private IP Addresses + NAT

Your whole house shares exactly one address as far as the internet is concerned — but every device inside still needs its own. Here's how your router keeps that straight.

IP address · public IP · private IP · NAT
01

One Building Address, Many Apartments

An apartment building has exactly one street address. Mail addressed to that building still has to reach the correct individual unit inside — that's the front desk's job, matching each letter to an apartment number. Your home network works the same way: your router has one public IP address that the entire internet sees, while every device inside your house gets its own private IP address that only matters inside your home.

02

NAT: the Router's Translation Table

NAT (Network Address Translation) is the job your router does every single time a device sends a request out, or a response comes back in. It keeps a table matching "which private device asked for what," so it knows exactly where to deliver the response.

Step 1

Request goes out

Your laptop (private IP) sends a request. The router swaps the private IP for its own public IP before sending it to the internet.

Step 2

Router remembers

The router logs in its translation table: "this specific request came from my laptop."

Step 3

Response comes back

The reply arrives addressed to the router's public IP — the only address the internet ever saw.

Step 4

Router delivers it

The router checks its table and forwards the response to the correct private device — your laptop, not your sibling's phone.

This is also why private IP addresses can repeat in every house on the planet.192.168.1.5 on your network and 192.168.1.5 on your neighbor's network never conflict, because the outside internet never sees either one — it only ever sees each house's one public IP.
03
