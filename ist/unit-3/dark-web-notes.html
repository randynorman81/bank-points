The Dark Web

A small, intentionally hidden corner of the Deep Web, built for one purpose: total anonymity. What you do with that anonymity is a completely separate question.

00

What It Is

The Dark Web is a small, intentionally hidden subset of the Deep Web — not a separate internet, just a specialized corner of it.

Intentional Concealment

Specifically designed for maximum anonymity — this isn't an accident of how it's built.

Specialized Software Required

Can't be accessed with a standard browser like Chrome or Firefox — you need tools like the Tor Browser.

A Tiny Fraction

Represents only a very small portion of the already-massive Deep Web.

Specialized Domains

Websites on the Tor network typically use the special top-level domain .onion, reachable only through the Tor browser.

02

The Two Sides of Anonymity

The high level of privacy on the Dark Web serves both legitimate and illicit purposes. The technology itself is neutral — how it's used is what determines legality.

Legitimate & Privacy-Focused

  • Journalists communicating with sources in dangerous situations
  • Activists and citizens in oppressive regimes organizing and accessing uncensored information (used during the Arab Spring)
  • Whistleblowers sharing sensitive information safely (WikiLeaks has a presence on the dark web)
  • Privacy-conscious individuals seeking protection from surveillance

Illicit & Criminal

  • Underground marketplaces selling illegal goods and services
  • Forums for selling stolen data, credentials, and malware
  • Hubs for ransomware-as-a-service operations

Built for one purpose — anonymity — used for every purpose imaginable.

The same three-relay hop that protects a journalist's source protects a criminal marketplace. The tool doesn't choose sides; the person using it does.
