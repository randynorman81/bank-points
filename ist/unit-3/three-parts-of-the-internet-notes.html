The 3 Parts of the Internet

Everything you've ever Googled is a tiny sliver of what's actually out there. This page is the map — click through to Surface, Deep, or Dark for the full deep-dive on each one.

00

The Web You Know Is Just the Tip of the Iceberg

Everything you can Google — every news site, every store, every social profile — is the small part sticking up above the waterline. Almost the entire internet is the massive, invisible part underneath.

Surface Web

~167 terabytes of data — the visible, searchable part everyone uses daily.

Deep Web

~92,000 terabytes of data — everything not indexed by a search engine. This is most of the internet.

Dark Web

A tiny, intentionally hidden slice inside the deep web, built specifically for anonymity.

Source: research estimates cited in "Exploiting the Deep Web with DynaBot" (Lyman & Varian, 2003).

Okay, But How Big Is That Actually?

Terabytes don't mean much until you turn them into something you'd actually do. Here's the same two numbers, translated into nonstop Netflix — HD streaming, no pausing, no sleeping, ever:

~167 TB

Surface Web

About 6 years of nonstop HD streaming to get through it all — basically your entire time in middle and high school combined, with the TV never once turning off.

~92,000 TB

Deep Web

About 3,500 years of nonstop streaming — longer than the time since the Great Pyramid of Giza was built. And that's the part of the internet you use every day.

550x bigger

The Gap

That's roughly how much bigger the Deep Web is than the Surface Web — everything you can Google is a rounding error next to it.

01

A Simple Framework: Public, Private, Secret

The easiest way to remember the difference is to think about access and intent — not how "scary" something sounds.

Public

Surface Web

Content that's entirely public. The creator doesn't care who accesses it — like sharing your name in a public place.

Private

Deep Web

Content that's private. Not shared with everyone, only specific people when it's needed — like telling a close friend a personal story.

Secret

Dark Web

Content that's actively hidden. You don't want anyone else to learn about it — like the password to your email account.
