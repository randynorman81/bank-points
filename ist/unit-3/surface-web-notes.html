The Surface Web

The internet you actually navigate every day — and a much smaller slice of the whole thing than it feels like.

00

What It Is

The portion of the Web that's readily available to the public and searchable with a standard search engine. Also called the Visible Web or Indexed Web.

A small slice

Estimated at less than 5–10% of all online content — nearly everything else is hidden from search engines.

Still enormous

As of 2015, Google's index of the Surface Web alone held roughly 14.8 billion pages.

01

How It Works: the World of the "Crawler"

Search engines like Google and Bing use automated programs called crawlers or spiders to discover and index content.

↻ The crawler repeats this loop constantly, discovering new pages by following links from pages it already knows.
Key term — robots.txt: a website can use this file to give instructions to crawlers, specifying exactly which pages should — or shouldn't — be indexed.
02

What Lives on the Surface?

Anything a search engine can add to its database. This is where most of your everyday online activity happens.

Information & News

BBC News, The New York Times, and every other public news outlet.

E-Commerce

Amazon, eBay, Shopify stores — anywhere you can shop.

Social Media

Public profiles and posts — anything not locked behind a private account.

Blogs & Forums

Public discussion boards and personal or professional blogs.

Corporate & Gov Sites

Official company pages and public government resources.

Nuance point: the login page for your email or bank is on the Surface Web — a crawler can find and index that page. But the content inside your account once you log in is not. That's the Deep Web's territory.

Public by design — and still less than 10% of everything out there.

Everything you'd normally call "the internet" fits inside this thin, visible layer. The other 90%+ is next.
