Emojis and Symbols

You have sent thousands of emojis. Now you will learn what they really are, and how to put them (and symbols like © ♥ →) on a web page you built yourself. Grab characters from the pad, style them, and then build your own page. The only skill you need is copy and paste.

Copy & Paste
Rule #1

It Is a Character, Not a Picture

Every emoji you have ever sent was never a photo. Your phone only sent a tiny code, and the other phone looked it up and drew a face. An emoji is closer to the letter A than to a photograph. That one idea explains everything on this page.

The first two are text, so they go in a heading, a paragraph, or a list item. The picture is a different thing and needs an img tag.

Because they are text, emojis and symbols follow the text rules you already know. You can put them in an h1, a p, an li, or a span. You size them with font-size and center them with text-align. They never need src, alt, or an upload.

01

Copy and Paste

There is only one trick, and you already know it. Find a character you like, copy it, click inside your code, and paste it. Once it is in your code, it is just text, like any letter.

Paste it between the opening and closing tags, with the other text. Never inside a tag's angle brackets.

Style Them Like Text

Since a heart is text, it can be styled like a word. But there is a twist: a symbol is a blank outline that takes on the text color, and an emoji is already finished artwork that ignores it. Slide, pick, and watch which ones change.

04

Same Code, Different Drawing

When you send a smiley, you send a code, and every device owns its own artist. Three artists, one code, three slightly different faces. A symbol like © hardly changes at all, but an emoji can.

Illustration: the same code, drawn three ways. This is why you should not depend on one exact look.

Good idea or bad idea?

A menu with a pizza emoji next to the word Pizza

Good

The emoji adds to the words. If it looks different on another phone, nothing is lost.

05

Common Mistakes

Wrong: an img tag for an emoji

<img src="smile.png" alt="smile">

Right: just paste it

<p>Hello 😀</p>

Wrong: pasted inside the tag

<h1 🍕>Pizza Night</h1>

Right: between the tags

<h1>Pizza Night 🍕</h1>

Wrong: a symbol as a style value

<p style="color: ★;">Winner</p>

Right: color name, symbol in the text

<p style="color: gold;">Winner ★</p>

Wrong: nothing but emojis

<p>🍕🥤🍰</p>

Right: words first

<p>Pizza 🍕 Taco 🥤 Pie 🍰</p>
Expecting color to change an emoji.It will not. Emojis keep their own colors. Use a symbol when you want to pick the color.
06

Vocab You Need

TermPlain-English meaning
CharacterOne letter, number, symbol, or emoji that you can type or paste as text
EmojiA small picture-like character, drawn by each device in its own style
SymbolA non-letter character like © ♥ → ° that takes on the text color
Copy and pasteThe way to put an emoji or symbol into your code: copy it, click between the tags, paste it
Text, not an imageAn emoji or symbol needs no img tag, no src, and no upload
font-sizeMakes any emoji or symbol bigger or smaller
colorChanges a symbol, but never an emoji
spanAn inline tag that styles just one character or a few words
