6.1 — Building a Knowledge Base

🔔 Bell Ringer — Think About It
  1. Today's notes admit you'll probably still be confused after your first draft of the KB article. Why would a good IT writer expect to fail on the first try instead of getting it right immediately?
  2. A KB article has to work for someone who knows nothing about computers. What's something you understand so well that you'd actually struggle to explain it simply to a total beginner?
  3. If two KB articles both technically "fix" a 404 error, but one is three sentences and one is ten clear steps, which one actually did its job better? Does shorter always mean better documentation?
  4. Companies pay IT writers to create these articles instead of just letting customer support explain things live every time. Why is writing it down once worth more than answering the same question a thousand times?

Directions

  1. Read the notes to learn what a knowledge base, a KB article, and IT support documentation are.
  2. Open the assignment. Name the parts of a KB article, compare a good one to a bad one, and fix the bad one.
  3. Write your own KB article that shows someone how to fix a 404 error.
  4. Have a classmate test your article, then finalize it and submit it in Google Classroom.

Notes

📄 What Is a KB Article? Notes

Write a KB (knowledge base) article about how to fix a 404 error. If you're still confused after your first draft (you probably will be, and that's normal), keep breaking it down further until you genuinely understand what it's explaining.
