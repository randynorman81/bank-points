6.2 — Star in a Video

🔔 Bell Ringer — Think About It
  1. You have to write a script and build a storyboard before you're allowed to touch a camera. Why not just hit record and figure it out as you go — what actually breaks in a video when you skip planning?
  2. Think of your favorite movie. If you only saw its storyboard (rough sketches, no sound, no acting) would you still be able to tell it was going to be good? What does that say about how much of "good" comes from the plan versus the performance?
  3. Being "the star" in your own video means being watched and judged by people you know. Is that harder or easier than performing for total strangers, and why?
  4. You have to make your project folder public for this assignment. Does knowing other people could see your rough drafts change how carefully you'd plan compared to if it were private the whole time?

Directions

You are making a KB article video: a screen-recorded walkthrough that shows a beginner how to fix or do something, step by step.

  1. Read the notes to learn how a KB article video is planned and made.
  2. Pick a topic and audience, then write the script.
  3. Create the storyboard (Storyboard That or a similar app).
  4. Record the video (Screencastify or similar). It must be 2 to 4 minutes and include all six parts of a KB article.
  5. Have a classmate test it, then put everything in a public Google folder and share the folder link with Mr. Norman.
