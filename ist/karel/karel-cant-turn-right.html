KAREL NOTES  /  LESSON 3

Karel Can't Turn Right

There is no turnRight(); command. This isn't a bug — it's the whole point of this lesson, and it sets up the biggest idea of the whole unit: building your own commands.

00

Wait… Karel Really Can't Turn Right?

Correct. Karel only knows one turning command: turnLeft();. There is no built-in turnRight();. If you need Karel to face right, you have to work it out using only turnLeft();.

Here's the trick: turning left three times in a row ends up facing the exact same direction as turning right once. Try picturing a clock face — three quarter-turns counter-clockwise lands you in the same spot as one quarter-turn clockwise.
turnLeft(); turnLeft(); turnLeft(); // three lefts = one right
01

How Many turnLeft(); Calls Do You Need?

What you wantHow many turnLeft() callsWhy
Turn right3Three quarter-turns left = one quarter-turn right
Turn around (face backward)2Two quarter-turns = a half-turn, either direction
Keep facing the same way4 (or 0)Four quarter-turns is a full circle, back where you started
Try it yourself: stand up and physically turn left three times. Notice you end up facing right. Your body is doing exactly what Karel does.
02

Why Would CodeHS Do This On Purpose?

This lesson isn't really about turning — it's setting up the single biggest idea in this whole unit.

The annoying part

Typing three turnLeft(); calls every single time you need to turn right gets old fast, and it's easy to lose count.

The fix, coming in Lesson 4

You'll learn to wrap those three lines up into your own new command — turnRight(); — that you define once and then use forever, as if it had been built in all along.

This is your very first taste of functions — giving a group of steps a name so you never have to repeat them. Lesson 4 makes it official.
03

Common Mistakes

Mistake 1

Writing two turnLeft() calls for a right turn

Two lefts turns Karel around backward, not right. You need three.

Mistake 2

Losing count in a long program

If Karel ends up facing the wrong way, count backward from where it should be facing — it's almost always a miscounted turnLeft();.

Three lefts make a right. Now let's stop repeating those three lines forever.

Next up: turning that trick into your own reusable command with functions.
