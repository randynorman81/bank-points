KAREL NOTES  /  LESSON 2

More Basic Karel

Same four commands from Lesson 1 — now you'll combine them into longer programs, read Karel's world more carefully, and start planning before you type.

00

Reading a Karel World

Before writing any commands, you have to be able to "read" the picture of Karel's world: where Karel starts, which way it's facing, where the walls are, and where any balls are.

Where is Karel?

Karel always starts on one specific corner, facing one specific direction (shown by the little arrow/triangle). Find that first, every single time.

What's in the way?

Walls block move();. Look for any thick lines between corners — those are walls Karel cannot walk through.

Habit to build now: before typing a single command, trace the path with your finger (or a pencil) on the picture first. Count exactly how many moves and turns it takes. Then type it.
01

Combining Commands: A Full Example

Here's a longer program that moves Karel forward, turns a corner, and drops off a ball — the same four commands from Lesson 1, just more of them in a row.

move(); move(); move(); turnLeft(); move(); putBall(); turnLeft(); turnLeft(); move(); takeBall();

Walk through it slowly, one line at a time: forward 3, turn, forward 1, drop a ball, turn around (two lefts in a row), forward 1, pick the ball back up. Every Karel program, no matter how long, is just this — a longer list of the same small steps.

Reading tip: two turnLeft(); calls in a row turns Karel a full 180° — facing the exact opposite direction. You'll use this pattern constantly.
02

Karel Can Only Carry So Much Information at Once

Karel doesn't remember anything about the world except what it's told to check with a command. It doesn't "know" it already visited a corner, or how many balls it's carrying, unless the program keeps track.

This means: if your plan depends on Karel "remembering" something, you have to write that into the commands yourself — Karel won't figure it out on its own.
03

Common Mistakes

Mistake 1

Trying to take a ball that isn't there

Calling takeBall(); on a corner with no ball crashes the program, the same way running into a wall does.

Mistake 2

Losing track of which way Karel faces

After a few turns, it's easy to lose track of Karel's direction in your head. Re-check the picture, or count your turns on your fingers.

Mistake 3

Writing the whole program before testing any of it

Test a few lines at a time when you can. Finding out line 3 was wrong is much easier than finding out somewhere in 20 lines something went wrong.

Longer programs are just more of the same small steps, in order.
