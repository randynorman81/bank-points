Chapter 01 — Start here

What even is "hacking" a password?

Every account you have — school, games, social media — is locked with one thing: a password. If someone can guess it, they get in and can pretend to be you. "Cracking" a password just means guessing it — either a person typing obvious guesses, or a computer trying millions of guesses a second. Today you'll use the real tools that do that guessing, safely, on made-up passwords — so you can see exactly how fast weak ones fall, and build ones that don't.

Length beats cleverness

A random string of words is easier to remember than "P@ssw0rd!23" — and usually far harder to crack, because it's longer. This kind of password is called a .

Aim for 16+ characters. Use a passphrase or a 's generator (Chapter 04) instead of memorized substitutions like @ for a () — Exhibit 05 shows exactly why those don't help as much as people think.

Quick checklist

  • 16+ characters, or a 4-5 word passphrase
  • Different password for every site — reuse is Exhibit 07's whole
