A Network of Networks

The Internet is not one computer in one building. It is a network of networks: your home network, your school network, and thousands of company networks, all connected and all following the same rules, called protocols. No one owns all of it.

Computing deviceA physical thing that can run a programlaptop, phone, game console, smart sensor

The Internet Is Not the Web

People say "the Internet" when they mean "the Web". They are not the same thing.

The InternetThe World Wide Web
What it isThe network: the cables, wireless links, routers, and the protocolsA service: linked pages and apps that you open with a browser
Rules it usesIP, TCP, UDPHTTP and HTTPS (and it runs on top of the Internet)
ExamplesEverything that travels over itwebsites, web apps
Other things that use the Internetemail, online games, video calls, streaming, texting apps(these are not the Web)
The Web is just one thing that runs on the Internet.If your browser is closed, the Internet is still working: your phone can still get a text or play an online game.
02

Bandwidth: How Many Lanes?

Picture the Internet as a giant highway. The roads are the cables and wireless links. Your data is the traffic. Bandwidth is the number of lanes: the most data that can travel at the same time. It is measured in bits per second: Mbps (millions) or Gbps (billions, so 1 Gbps is 1,000 Mbps).

VehicleActivityRoughly how many lanes it takes
Motorcyclereading a text page, music, a text message1 Mbps or less
CarHD video, a video call, an online game3 to 10 Mbps
Semi-trailera 4K movie, a huge download25 Mbps or more

The lanes are shared by every device in the house. When too many big vehicles are on the road at once, the lanes fill up, traffic backs up, and everything gets slow. That is the buffering circle on a video.

Try it: the traffic jam

Pick your plan (the number of lanes), then switch on what your house is doing at the same time.

More bandwidth = more lanes. It lets more data move at the same time, but it does not make one trip shorter. See the next part.
03

Bandwidth Versus Latency

These two ideas get mixed up all the time.

BandwidthLatency
It measuresHow much data can move at onceHow long one trip takes (a delay)
Highway pictureThe number of lanesHow long the road is, and the speed limit
Unitsbits per second (Mbps, Gbps)time (milliseconds, ms)
Matters most forbig downloads, 4K videoonline games, video calls
Example: Adding lanes (more bandwidth) lets more cars travel together, but a car still needs the same time to drive from one town to the other. A far-away server has more latency than a nearby one, even on a fast plan.
04

How the Internet Gets Into Your House

A company called an ISP (Internet Service Provider, like AT&T or Verizon) owns a huge network of cables. To get you online, the ISP runs a cable, often fiber optic (data sent as pulses of light), from the street into your home.

Modem = the front door

It brings the Internet in from the ISP's cable. It is the doorway between your house and the Internet.

Router = the streets inside

It shares that one connection with every device, over Wi-Fi and cables. Like the streets inside a neighborhood.

Many home boxes are both a modem and a router in one case, but the two jobs are different: the modem brings the Internet in, the router shares it.
05

How Long Will It Take?

Speed is bandwidth, and bandwidth is in bits. Files are measured in bytes. 1 byte = 8 bits. So change the file to bits first, then divide by the speed.

Example: a 200 MB game update. 200 × 8 = 1,600 megabits. At 100 Mbps: 1,600 ÷ 100 = 16 seconds.

Try it: how long will the download take?

Files are measured in bytes. Bandwidth is measured in bits. Multiply the file by 8 first.

Watch the capital letters. Mbps with a small b is megabits per second (the plan). MB with a capital B is megabytes (the file). That is a factor of 8.

Which statement describes the Internet best?

The Internet is a network of networks that follow open protocols. The Web is just one service that runs on it.

A family has a 50 Mbps plan. At the same time they stream two 4K movies (25 Mbps each) and make a video call (3 Mbps). What is most likely?

25 + 25 + 3 = 53 Mbps. That is more lanes than the plan gives, so traffic backs up.

How long does a 600 MB file take to download at 100 Mbps?

600 MB × 8 = 4,800 megabits. 4,800 ÷ 100 = 48 seconds. (6 seconds forgets to multiply by 8.)

In the highway picture, which device is the front door that brings the Internet into your house?

The modem connects to the ISP's cable. The router shares the connection inside the house.

Which is true about bandwidth and latency?

Bandwidth is the number of lanes. Latency is the delay. More lanes does not make one car arrive sooner.
