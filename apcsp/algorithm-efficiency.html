What Is Efficiency?

An algorithm's efficiency is how much time and resources it needs, usually described by how the number of steps grows as the input gets bigger. It is not about the stopwatch. A faster computer makes every algorithm faster, but it does not change how the steps grow.

AlgorithmSteps for a list of n itemsDoubling the list…
Linear searchup to ndoubles the steps
Binary search (sorted list)about log2 nadds one step
Two nested loops over the listn × nmakes four times the steps

The exam does not ask you to count exact steps. It asks you to compare two algorithms and say which grows faster.

01

Reasonable and Unreasonable Time

Computer scientists split algorithms into two families.

Try it: how fast does the work grow?

A computer does about one billion steps per second. Slide n (the size of the input) and see how long each kind of algorithm needs.

Reasonable timeUnreasonable time
Number of steps grows like…n, n², n³ (a polynomial), or slower like log n2n or n! (exponential or factorial)
As the input grows…the time grows slowly enough to finishthe time explodes and soon takes longer than anyone can wait
Typical examplesearching a list, sorting, nested loopstrying every combination or every ordering
Unreasonable does not mean impossible. It means that for large inputs, the algorithm would take so long it is useless, even on the fastest computers.
02

Decision Problems and Optimization Problems

Problems come in two common shapes.

Decision problemOptimization problem
Asksa yes or no questionfind the best answer
Example"Is 17 in this list?" "Is there a route under 100 miles?""What is the shortest route that visits every stop?"

Many optimization problems (like the shortest route through every stop) have no known reasonable-time algorithm that is guaranteed to find the best answer.

03

Heuristics: Good Enough, Fast Enough

When finding the best answer takes unreasonable time, a heuristic finds a good, but not guaranteed best, answer in reasonable time. It is a rule of thumb.

Try it: a delivery driver with seven stops

A driver must visit every stop and come back to the start. The best route means checking every order. The heuristic ("always go to the nearest stop") is quick, but not always the best.

HeuristicUsed forTrade-off
go to the nearest stop nextdelivery routesfast, sometimes noticeably longer than the best
suggest roads that are usually fastGPS navigationquick answer, not always the very fastest
flag an email with words like "winner" and "urgent"spam filterscatches most spam, but misses some and flags some real mail

AP

On the AP Exam

These are the ideas the exam asks about. Be ready to explain each one in your own words.

  • Efficiency describes how the number of steps grows with the size of the input, not how many seconds one run takes.
  • Polynomial growth (n, n², n³) is reasonable. Exponential and factorial growth (2n, n!) is unreasonable.
  • A decision problem asks yes/no. An optimization problem asks for the best.
  • A heuristic finds a good (not guaranteed best) answer in reasonable time when the best answer takes unreasonable time.
  • Comparing algorithms is about which one grows faster, not exact step counts.

Grows slowly: fine. Explodes: use a heuristic.
