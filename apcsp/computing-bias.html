What Is Computing Bias?

Bias means a computer system gives systematically unfair results to some people or groups. Computers do exactly what they are told, so it is tempting to think they cannot be biased. But people choose the data, write the rules, and decide what counts as success, and their assumptions and blind spots can end up inside the program.

Exam idea: computing innovations can reflect existing human biases because of the data used to build them. Bias can be unintentional, and it can repeat or increase unfairness that already exists.
01

Where Does Bias Come From?

Bias gets in at several points.

DATA

Biased training data

A face-recognition program trained mostly on light-skinned faces works worse on dark-skinned faces.

PAST

Historical bias

A hiring program trained on past hires copies the unfair patterns in who got hired before.

DESIGN

Design choices

A designer picks what to measure. A "risk score" that uses zip code can stand in for race.

TEAM

Lack of diversity

If everyone on the team is alike, nobody notices who the product fails.

02

Real Examples

These happened in real systems.

SystemWhat went wrongWhy
Face recognitionmore errors for women and for people with darker skintraining photos were not diverse
Resume screeningranked men's resumes higher for technical jobslearned from a company's past hiring
Voice assistantsunderstood some accents far less wellmostly trained on a few accents
Loan or insurance scoringcharged some neighborhoods moreused zip code as a stand-in for race or income
Feedback loops: if a biased program decides where police patrol or who gets a loan, the results become new data, which trains the next version to be even more biased.
03

What Can Be Done?

Bias cannot be removed by good intentions alone. Teams use specific steps.

StepWhat it does
Diverse, representative datamake sure every group appears in the training data
Test on every groupmeasure accuracy for each group separately, not just overall
Diverse teamsmore people who notice problems early
Human review and appealsa person can check and fix a decision
Be openexplain how the system decides, so outsiders can check it

Check yourself

Answer each one. You see why right away, so read the explanation even when you are right.

A hiring program trained on a company's past hires keeps favoring candidates who look like previous employees. What is the most likely cause?

The program learned the pattern in its data. Computers copy human bias in the data they are given.

A face-recognition program works well on most people but often fails for a small group. Which action would best address this?

Fix the data and test every group, not just the overall score.

A program is 92% accurate overall. Why might that number be misleading?

A big majority group can make the overall number high while a minority group gets poor results.

Which is an example of bias that was not intended by the programmers?

Bias is often unintentional: no one wrote an unfair rule, but the data did the damage.
AP

On the AP Exam

These are the ideas the exam asks about. Be ready to explain each one in your own words.

  • Bias in computing means systematically unfair results for some people. It can come from data, design choices, and team makeup.
  • Computing innovations can reflect and even increase existing human bias. The bias is often unintentional.
  • Using diverse, representative data and testing every group helps reduce bias.
  • A high overall accuracy can hide poor results for a smaller group.

Computers copy the world they are shown.
