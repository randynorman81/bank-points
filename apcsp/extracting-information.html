Data Is Not the Same as Information

Data is the raw values: numbers, text, measurements. Information is what you learn from the data. A list of 10,000 temperatures is data. "It has been getting warmer every year" is information.

DataInformation
What it israw facts and valuesa conclusion drawn from the facts
Example12 monthly sales totalssales are highest in July and lowest in January
How you get itsensors, forms, logs, surveyscalculating, comparing, and looking for patterns

Computers are good at this because they can process far more data than a person can read, and they do it the same way every time. A program can sort, count, average, and compare the data to extract information.

01

Patterns and Trends

A pattern is something that repeats or goes together. A trend is a pattern over time: values that keep rising, keep falling, or rise and fall in a cycle.

Kind of patternExampleWhat it can tell you
Rising trenda town's average temperature each yearthe climate is warming
Seasonal cycleice cream sales by monthdemand follows the seasons
Group differencetest scores by hours of sleepstudents who sleep more score higher
Outlierone day with 10,000 website visitorsa one-time event, or an error to check

Larger data sets give more reliable trends. Three data points can be a coincidence. Three million are much harder to dismiss.

02

Correlation Is Not Causation

Two things are correlated when they change together. That does not mean one causes the other. Often a third factor drives both: a confounding variable.

Try it: do these two things move together?

Twelve months of made-up data for one town. Pick any two columns and see the pattern and the correlation number.

The classic mistake: "Ice cream sales and sunburns rise together, so ice cream causes sunburns." Really, hot weather causes both.

03

Outliers and Averages

One extreme value can pull an average a long way. When data has an outlier, the median (the middle value) can describe it better than the mean (the average).

DataMeanMedianWhat happened
10, 12, 11, 13, 141212the two agree
10, 12, 11, 13, 500109.212the 500 pulls the mean up. The median stays put.
An outlier is either a real, interesting value (a record-breaking day) or an error (a typo). Look into it before you decide.

AP

On the AP Exam

These are the ideas the exam asks about. Be ready to explain each one in your own words.

  • Information is extracted from data by finding patterns, trends, and relationships. Programs can do this with far more data than a person could.
  • A trend is a pattern over time. Bigger data sets give more reliable trends.
  • Correlation does not imply causation. A confounding variable can drive both.
  • Outliers can change an average. Decide if they are real or errors.

Data is the question. Information is the answer.
