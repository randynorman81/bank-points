What Is Crowdsourcing?

Crowdsourcing means getting ideas, work, data, or money from a large group of people, usually over the Internet. The Internet makes it possible to reach thousands or millions of people who each give a small piece.

Citizen Science

In citizen science, ordinary people help scientists collect or analyze data. A scientist could never watch every bird in the country, but a million bird watchers with phones can report what they see.

Example: A scientist wants to know where a bird species is moving as the climate changes. Thousands of volunteers report sightings in an app. The researcher gets data that would take one team a lifetime to collect.
02

Why Crowds Can Be Wise

Each person's guess is off in a different direction. When you combine many independent guesses, the errors tend to cancel out.

Try it: the wisdom of the crowd

The jar below holds 500 jelly beans. A crowd of people each guess. A single guess can be far off, but what happens to the average of many guesses?

Slide to 1, then to 200. A single guess is often far off. The average of many is usually close. Then tick the box: a shared bias cannot cancel out.

Benefits and Concerns

Crowdsourcing is powerful, but it has real problems.

Benefits

Scale: huge amounts of work or data. Variety: many viewpoints and skills. Speed and low cost. It can solve problems too big for one team.

Concerns

Quality: anyone can be wrong or add vandalism. Bias: the crowd may not represent everyone. Manipulation: fake reviews and bots. Fairness: who gets credit or pay? Privacy of contributors.
