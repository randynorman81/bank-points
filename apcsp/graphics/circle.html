Circle

Made from one number — a radius — and positioned by its center. That single difference from Rectangle causes most Circle bugs.

00

Definition

A Circle is created with one number: its radius, the distance from the center to the edge. set_position(x, y) places the center of the circle at that point — not a corner, not the edge.

circle = Circle(50) # radius 50 circle.set_position(100, 200) # CENTER goes at (100, 200) circle.set_color(Color.red) add(circle)
Because Circle is positioned by its center, a 50px-radius circle at (100, 200) actually stretches from x=50 to x=150 and from y=150 to y=250 — 50 pixels in every direction from that point.

Centering a circle on the canvas

Because set_position() already means "put the center here," centering a circle is the simplest shape to center — you don't need to account for its size at all:

circle = Circle(30) x = get_width() / 2 y = get_height() / 2 circle.set_position(x, y) # dead center, on any size canvas add(circle)
Why divide canvas size by the shape's own size? Using get_width() and get_height() instead of typed-in numbers means this code still centers the circle correctly even if you resize the canvas later with set_size().
02

Common Problems / Errors

Treating set_position() like Rectangle's: Rectangle positions its top-left corner; Circle positions its center. Code copied from a Rectangle example and reused for a Circle will place it in the wrong spot — often partly off the edge of the canvas.

Radius vs. diameter

Circle(50) makes a circle 100px across, not 50 — the argument is the radius, half the total width.

Forgetting add()

Same rule as every shape: creating and positioning a Circle without calling add(circle) leaves the canvas blank, with no error.
