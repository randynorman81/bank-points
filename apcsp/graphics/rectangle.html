Rectangle

Made from two numbers — width and height — and positioned by its top-left corner. That single difference from Circle causes most Rectangle bugs.

00

Definition

A Rectangle is created with two numbers: width and height. set_position(x, y) places the top-left corner of the rectangle at that point — not the center.

rect = Rectangle(200, 50) # 200 wide, 50 tall rect.set_position(100, 200) # TOP-LEFT CORNER goes at (100, 200) rect.set_color(Color.blue) add(rect)
Because Rectangle is positioned by its corner, a 200×50 rectangle placed at (100, 200) stretches from x=100 to x=300, and from y=200 to y=250 — growing right and down from that point, never up or left.

Truly centering a rectangle

Unlike Circle, centering a Rectangle takes an extra step — because set_position() sets the corner, you have to shift it back by half the rectangle's own width and height first, or it'll be centered "too far down and right."

rect = Rectangle(200, 50) # naive version -- puts the CORNER at center, not the rectangle rect.set_position(get_width() / 2, get_height() / 2) # truly centered -- uses the rectangle's own width and height x = get_width() / 2 - rect.get_width() / 2 y = get_height() / 2 - rect.get_height() / 2 rect.set_position(x, y)
This is exactly why get_width() and get_height() exist on a shape. You need to know how big the rectangle actually is before you can correctly offset it away from a corner-based position.
02

Common Problems / Errors

Treating set_position() like Circle's: Circle positions its center; Rectangle positions its top-left corner. Code copied from a Circle example and reused for a Rectangle will place it shifted down and to the right of where you meant.

No set_width() / set_height()

Rectangle has get_width()/get_height() to read its size, but no matching setters — you can't resize one after creating it. If you need a different size, make a new Rectangle.

Running off the canvas

Because it grows right and down from its corner, a rectangle placed near the bottom-right edge can easily extend past get_width()/get_height() without any warning.

Spotify Examples (5)

Five rectangular elements from a real music app, all built the same way — a width, a height, and a top-left corner.

01

The progress bar

bar = Rectangle(get_width() - 40, 6) bar.set_position(20, 180)

Its width comes from the canvas's own width, so it always spans edge to edge with 20px margins.

02

Album art thumbnail

art = Rectangle(80, 80) art.set_position(20, 20)

Equal width and height — a square, made from a Rectangle.

03

A playlist row highlight

row = Rectangle(get_width(), 40) row.set_position(0, 120)

Full canvas width, positioned by its top-left corner at the very left edge.

04

Checking how much room is left

used = art.get_x() + art.get_width() remaining = get_width() - used

Combines the art's own position and size with the canvas's width to find open space.

05

Centering the progress bar

bar = Rectangle(300, 6) x = get_width() / 2 - bar.get_width() / 2 bar.set_position(x, 180)

True centering — offsetting by half the bar's own width before placing it.

Two numbers in. One corner. Grows right and down from there.

Every Rectangle question comes down to remembering that set_position() means the corner, not the middle.
