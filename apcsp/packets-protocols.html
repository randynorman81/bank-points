Why Packets?

The Internet breaks every message into small chunks called packets. A photo you send to a friend is chopped up, sent in different directions, and put back together at the other end.

1

Sharing

Many people can share the same cables, because their packets take turns.

2

Resending

If one piece is lost, only that piece is resent, not the whole file.

3

Detours

Packets can travel by different routes around problems.

01

Headers and Payloads

A packet is more than a piece of data. It is like an envelope. The payload is the piece of your message inside. The header is the information written on the outside: the source and destination addresses and a sequence number ("packet 2 of 3") so the receiver can put things back in order.

02

Packet Switching and Reassembly

Packets from the same message can take different routes and arrive out of order. This is called packet switching. The receiver uses the sequence numbers to reassemble the message correctly.

Try it: send a message as packets

Type a message and choose how big each packet is. Then send it. Packets can arrive out of order, and one might get lost.

03

TCP and UDP

Sometimes a packet is lost because a busy router drops it. Different protocols handle that differently.

TCP (Transmission Control Protocol)UDP (User Datagram Protocol)
Reliable?Yes. The receiver acknowledges what arrived and the sender resends anything missing.No. It sends and does not check.
Speeda little slower (the checking takes time)faster, less delay
Good forweb pages, email, file downloads (everything must arrive)live video, voice calls, online games (a late packet is useless anyway)
04

Protocols and Open Standards

A protocol is a set of agreed rules that lets different devices communicate. Your phone, a school server, and a game console are made by different companies, but they all follow the same rules.

ProtocolIts job
IPaddresses and routing: gets each packet toward its destination
TCP / UDPhow packets are delivered (reliable, or fast)
HTTP / HTTPSweb pages (HTTPS is the encrypted version)
SMTPemail
DNSturning names into IP addresses
DHCPgiving devices an address when they join a network
Internet protocols are open standards: published for anyone to use.That is why the Internet scales (grows without breaking): a new device or network only has to follow the rules.
05

Where the Web Fits

The Web is built on top of all of this. When you open a page, your browser sends an HTTP request ("give me this page") inside TCP packets, and the server sends back an HTTP response (the page).

Check yourself

Answer each one. You see why right away, so read the explanation even when you are right.

What does the sequence number in a packet's header help the receiver do?
Packets can arrive out of order, so the receiver sorts them by sequence number.
Which protocol resends lost packets?
TCP is reliable: it acknowledges packets and resends missing ones.
A game sends your position 60 times every second. Which is the better choice, and why?
For live data, speed beats perfection. UDP does not wait for resends.
Why do Internet protocols being open standards help the Internet grow?
Open standards let the Internet scale. A new device just follows the published rules.
Why is a message split into packets?
Sharing, resending only what was lost, and using different routes are the three big reasons.
AP

On the AP Exam

These are the ideas the exam asks about. Be ready to explain each one in your own words.

  • Data is sent as packets. Each has a header (addresses, sequence number) and a payload (part of the data).
  • Packets can take different routes and arrive out of order. The receiver reassembles them.
  • TCP is reliable and resends. UDP is faster but does not check.
  • A protocol is an agreed set of rules. Open standards let the Internet scale.
  • HTTP/HTTPS is the protocol of the Web. The Web runs on the Internet, not the other way around.

Small pieces, shared roads, agreed rules.
