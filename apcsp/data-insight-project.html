What Is a Data-Driven Insight?

An insight is something you learned from data that was not obvious before, stated clearly and backed up by evidence. "Students who slept 8 or more hours averaged 17 points higher on the test" is an insight. "Sleep is important" is just an opinion.

PartWhat it isExample
Questionwhat you wanted to find outDo students who sleep more score higher?
Datawhere the evidence came froma survey of 120 students, with sleep and test scores
Findingwhat the numbers showthe 8-hour group averaged 89.6, the others 73.0
Visuala chart that shows the finding at a glancea bar chart of the two averages
Limitwhat the data cannot tell youit shows a link, not that sleep causes higher scores

02

Choosing a Data Set

A good data set makes the project easy. A bad one makes it impossible.

Look for

A credible source (a government agency, a university, a respected organization). Enough rows to see a pattern (dozens at least). Clear columns with units. A date or version. Permission or an open license to use it.

Avoid

Data with names or other PII about real people. Unknown sources. Tiny samples. Data that cannot answer your question. Data collected in a way that is obviously biased.

KindExamples
Government and scienceweather and climate data, census and public health numbers
Sports and gamesseason statistics, scores, player records
School and communitya survey you collect yourself, with consent and no names
Technologyapp usage numbers, public data about the Internet
03

Write the Insight

Before you build any slides, write the insight as one or two sentences and check it.

Try it: is it a strong insight statement?

An insight is a claim the data supports. Fill in the three parts and see what a reviewer would say.

Correlation is not causation. If your data only shows two things move together, say "are linked" or "go together", not "causes".

The Presentation

Keep it short. Every slide earns its place.

SlideWhat it says
1. The questionwhat you asked and why it matters
2. The datawhere it came from, how many rows, and how you cleaned it
3. The methodwhat you calculated (a short Python snippet or a plain-words description)
4. The chartone honest chart with a title, labels, and units
5. The insightyour one-sentence finding, with the number
6. Limits and next stepswhat the data cannot show, and what you would do next
Sourcescredit for the data and any code you borrowed

A student finds that students who play more video games have higher test scores and writes, "Video games make students smarter." What is the main problem?
That is a causation claim made from a correlation.
Which data set is the best choice for the project?
A credible source, enough rows, and clear columns. Personal information should be avoided.
Which step should come before making the chart?
Charts of dirty data give wrong pictures.
AP

On the AP Exam

These are the ideas the exam asks about. Be ready to explain each one in your own words.

  • An insight is a specific, evidence-backed finding that answers a question.
  • The process: ask, find data, clean, analyze, visualize, tell the story, cite.
  • State the limits of the data and do not claim causation from a correlation.
  • Choose credible, sufficient, non-personal data, and credit your sources.
