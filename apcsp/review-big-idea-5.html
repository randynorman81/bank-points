Review: Big Idea 5, Impact of Computing

How computing changes the world: its good and bad effects, the digital divide, bias, ownership, privacy, and security. Review the terms, then test yourself.

00

The Summary

Big Idea 5: Impact of Computing is about how computing affects people and society. These are mostly reasoning questions, and many use a short reading.

TermMeaning
Beneficial / harmful effectsmost innovations have both; effects can be intended or unintended
Digital divideunequal access to devices and the Internet by income, place, or group
Crowdsourcinggetting input from many people online; citizen science is one kind
Biassystematically unfair results, often from biased data or design
Copyright / Creative Commonsautomatic ownership / a license that allows certain uses in advance
Open sourcesource code is public and can be used under a license
PIIinformation that can identify, contact, or locate a person
MFAmultifactor authentication: two or more kinds of proof
Phishing, malware, keyloggera tricking message; harmful software; software that records keystrokes
Public key encryptiona public key encrypts, a private key decrypts
Rogue access point, DDoSfake Wi-Fi; many devices flooding a site

Practice Set (10 questions)

Think about who is affected and why.

Check yourself

Answer each one. You see why right away, so read the explanation even when you are right.

A smartphone app was designed to help people find parking. Its location data is also sold to advertisers, who use it to track where people go. This is an example of
The app also has an effect its users may not expect and may not want.
Which two would most directly help reduce the digital divide? select TWO
Both increase access. The other two make access harder.
A news site asks readers to submit photos of storm damage, which scientists then use to map the storm's path. This is an example of
A large group contributes data online: crowdsourcing (citizen science).
A face-recognition program works well on most faces but poorly on a small group. Which action would best reduce the problem?
Improve the data and test every group, not just the overall score.
A photographer shares a photo with a CC BY-NC license. Which use is allowed?
BY requires credit. NC means no commercial use.
Which is an example of PII?
A name with an address identifies and locates a specific person.
A text says, "Your account will be locked! Reply with your password now." The sender's address looks slightly wrong. This is most likely
A message that tricks you into giving up private information is phishing.
A site requires a password and a code sent to the user's phone. Why does this make an account more secure?
Two different kinds of proof: something you know and something you have.
Which two statements about public key encryption are true? select TWO
Anyone can encrypt with the public key. Only the private key decrypts. That is why no secret needs to be shared first.

On the AP Exam

If you missed any, reread the Unit 11 and Unit 16 notes.

  • Describe beneficial and harmful, intended and unintended effects, and who they affect.
  • Explain the digital divide, bias, and crowdsourcing with an example.
  • Know copyright, Creative Commons, open source, and plagiarism.
  • Know PII, MFA, phishing, malware, keyloggers, rogue access points, and DDoS, and what protects you.

Who is affected, and why?
