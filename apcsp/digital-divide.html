What Is the Digital Divide?

The digital divide is the gap between people who have access to computers and the Internet and people who do not. It is not just "has Internet or not". The quality of the connection, the devices, and the skills to use them matter too.

Exam wording: access to computing devices and the Internet is not equal across different socioeconomic, geographic, and demographic groups.
Kind of gapWhat it meansExample
Socioeconomicdifferences in income and educationa family that cannot afford a laptop or a monthly plan
Geographicwhere you liverural areas where no company has run fiber
Demographicage, disability, language, and other groupsolder adults, people who use screen readers, speakers of other languages
01

Why Does It Happen?

Several things pile up.

COST

Money

Devices, home plans, and repairs cost money many families do not have.

PLACE

Infrastructure

Companies build fast networks where many customers live, so rural and remote areas get slower service or none.

SKILLS

Digital literacy

Having a device is not enough if no one has shown you how to use it safely and well.

DESIGN

Accessibility

Websites that do not work with screen readers, or are only in one language, leave people out.

02

What Does It Cost People?

Because so much of life moved online, a gap in access becomes a gap in opportunity.

Try it: how long does a school task take on different connections?

Pick a connection and a task. The same task is quick for one student and nearly impossible for another.

AreaEffect of lacking access
Educationthe "homework gap": students who cannot do online assignments or research at home

Civic lifeharder to find information about voting, government services, and news

Which best describes the digital divide?
It is a gap in access that follows socioeconomic, geographic, and demographic lines.
A student has no Internet at home and cannot do online homework. Which is the most likely effect?
This is the homework gap: a lack of access becomes a gap in opportunity.
Which would best reduce the geographic part of the digital divide?
Rural areas often lack infrastructure. Building networks there directly closes the geographic gap.
A website only works with a mouse and has no text alternatives for images. Who is most likely to be left out?
Poor accessibility widens the demographic divide. Designing for accessibility is part of the fix.
AP

On the AP Exam

These are the ideas the exam asks about. Be ready to explain each one in your own words.

  • The digital divide is the gap in access to computing devices and the Internet across socioeconomic, geographic, and demographic groups.
  • It affects education, jobs, health care, and civic life.
  • Access is more than a device: it includes the connection quality, skills, and accessible design.
  • Ways to reduce it include public access, lower costs, building infrastructure, and accessible design.
