Real Data Is Messy

Data collected from people and sensors is rarely perfect. Cleaning data means finding and fixing (or removing) the problems before you analyze it. If you skip it, your answers can be wrong, a rule called garbage in, garbage out.

ProblemExampleUsual fix
Missing valuesa survey row with no ageleave it out, or fill it in sensibly (and say so)
Duplicatesthe same sign-up entered twicekeep one copy
Inconsistent formatGA, ga, Georgia, "GA "pick one format and convert the rest
Impossible valuesan age of 200, a negative pricecheck the source, then correct or remove
Mixed unitssome heights in inches, others in centimetersconvert everything to one unit

02

Cleaning Can Change the Answer

Cleaning decisions are choices, and choices have consequences.

Keep the original

Never overwrite the raw data. Work on a copy so you can check or redo your cleaning.

Write down what you did

Record every rule ("removed ages over 120"). Others need to trust your result.

Dropping rows can create bias. If most of the missing ages come from one group (say, younger students who skipped the question), removing them changes who is represented.

On the AP Exam

These are the ideas the exam asks about. Be ready to explain each one in your own words.

  • Data must often be cleaned before it is used: missing values, duplicates, inconsistent formats, and impossible values.
  • Garbage in, garbage out: conclusions are only as good as the data.
  • Cleaning decisions can introduce bias. Keep the original and document what you changed.
  • Programs clean data with loops and conditionals.
