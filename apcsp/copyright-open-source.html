Who Owns a Creation?

Intellectual property is something a person creates with their mind: a song, a photo, a story, a program. Creators have legal rights over it. Copyright is the legal protection that gives the creator the right to say who may copy, share, and change the work. It applies automatically the moment the work is created.

WordMeaning
Copyrightlegal ownership of a creative work. Others need permission to copy or share it.
Licensethe permission the owner gives. It states what others may do.
Fair uselimited use without permission for things like criticism, news, teaching, or research. It depends on how much, and why.
Plagiarismpresenting someone else's work as your own. It is an ethics rule, even when no law is broken.
Piracycopying or sharing protected work (movies, music, software) without permission.
Public domainworks with no copyright left. Anyone may use them.
01

Computing Makes Copying Easy

A digital copy is perfect, free, and takes a second. That makes it very easy to break copyright by accident, and very hard for creators to stop it.

Legal and ethical

Something can be legal but unethical (copying an idea so closely that it takes credit) or ethical but illegal (sharing a file to help a friend). The exam asks you to think about both.

Citing your sources

Give credit when you use someone else's work: images, code, and ideas. In the Create Task you must credit any code you did not write in a comment.

02

Creative Commons

Creators who want people to share their work can give permission in advance with a Creative Commons (CC) license. It is still copyrighted, but it tells everyone exactly what they may do.

03

Open Source and Open Access

Open-source software has its source code published so anyone can read it, use it, change it, and share it, under a license. Open access means research and content is free to read.

Proprietary softwareOpen-source software
Source codekept secret by the companypublished for anyone to read
Who can change itonly the companyanyone (under the license)
ExamplesMicrosoft Office, most phone appsLinux, Firefox, Python
Proscompany support, a single planfree to use, many eyes find bugs, anyone can improve it
Conscost; you cannot fix it yourselfmay have less support; the license still has rules
Open source is not "no rules". Each project has a license. Some (like the GPL) require your changes to be shared under the same license. Others (like MIT) only require you to credit the original author.

Check yourself

Answer each one. You see why right away, so read the explanation even when you are right.

A student copies a photo from a website into a presentation without credit or permission. What is the main problem?

Copyright protects the photo automatically. Using it without permission or credit is a legal and ethical problem.

A musician publishes a song with a CC BY-NC license. Which use is allowed?

BY-NC requires credit and allows non-commercial use only.

What does it mean for software to be open source?

The source code is available. A license still sets rules for use.

Which is the best example of fair use?

Fair use is limited: a small amount, for something like criticism or education.

Which statement about copyright is true?

Creators get copyright automatically. Being online does not make a work free to use.
AP

On the AP Exam

These are the ideas the exam asks about. Be ready to explain each one in your own words.

  • Copyright gives creators rights over their work automatically. Using it without permission can be illegal and unethical.
  • Creative Commons licenses let creators allow certain uses in advance. Know BY (credit), NC (no commercial), ND (no changes), and SA (share alike).
  • Open-source software has public source code. Open access content is free to read. Both still have licenses.
  • Plagiarism is presenting someone else's work as yours. Always credit the code, images, and ideas you did not create.
  • Digital copying is easy, so legal and ethical questions come up constantly.

Make it, credit it, and read the license.
